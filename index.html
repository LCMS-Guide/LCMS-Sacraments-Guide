What the LCMS teaches about baptism

In the words of the Lutheran Confessions and the LCMS.

What baptism is

In short summary, not a quoteBaptism is water joined to God's command and His Word.

“Baptism is not simple water only, but it is the water included in God’s command and connected with God’s Word.”

Small Catechism, Baptism

Baptism is necessary

In short summary, not a quoteChrist commands baptism, and God offers His grace through it, so the Lutheran Confessions call it necessary for salvation.

“Concerning Baptism, our churches teach that Baptism is necessary for salvation [Mark 16:16] and that God’s grace is offered through Baptism [Titus 3:4–7].”

Augsburg Confession, Art. IX, ¶1

What baptism gives

In short summary, not a quoteBaptism forgives sins, rescues from death and the devil, and gives eternal salvation to everyone who believes God's promise.

“It works forgiveness of sins, delivers from death and the devil, and gives eternal salvation to all who believe this, as the words and promises of God declare.”

Small Catechism, Baptism

How water can do this

In short summary, not a quoteThe water has no power by itself. God's Word joined to it does the work, and faith trusts that Word.

“It is not the water indeed that does them, but the Word of God, which is in and with the water, and faith, which trusts this Word of God in the water. For without the Word of God the water is simple water and no Baptism.”

Small Catechism, Baptism

God is the one baptizing

In short summary, not a quoteHuman hands, usually a pastor's, do the baptizing, but God is the one acting. In an emergency any Christian may baptize.

“To be baptized in God’s name is to be baptized not by men, but by God Himself. Therefore, although it is performed by human hands, it is still truly God’s own work.”

Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶10

Faith receives it

In short summary, not a quoteFaith doesn't make a baptism real. It receives what God gives.

“For my faith does not make Baptism, but receives it.”

Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶53

Children are to be baptized

In short summary, not a quoteChildren should be baptized, because through baptism God receives them into His grace.

“They teach that children are to be baptized [Acts 2:38–39]. Being offered to God through Baptism, they are received into God’s grace.”

Augsburg Confession, Art. IX, ¶2

More than a mark of profession

In short summary, not a quoteSacraments aren't mainly our public statement of faith. They are God's signs of His will toward us, given to create and strengthen faith.

“Our churches teach that the Sacraments were ordained, not only to be marks of profession among men, but even more, to be signs and testimonies of God’s will toward us. They were instituted to awaken and confirm faith in those who use them.”

Augsburg Confession, Art. XIII, ¶1–2

One baptism

In short summary, not a quoteBaptism is never repeated. Its work goes on every day, so being baptized again would add nothing.

“But we do not need to be sprinkled with water again [Ezekiel 36:25–26; Hebrews 10:22]. Even if we were put under the water a hundred times, it would still be only one Baptism, even though the work and sign continue and remain.”

Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶78

A daily return

In short summary, not a quoteEach day, through repentance, the old sinful self dies and a new person rises to live for God.

“It signifies that the old Adam in us should, by daily contrition and repentance, be drowned and die with all sins and evil lusts. And also it shows that a new man should daily come forth and arise, who shall live before God in righteousness and purity forever.”

Small Catechism, Baptism

How much water

In short summary, not a quoteImmersion, pouring, and sprinkling are all valid. How the water is applied doesn't change baptism's blessings.

“Strictly speaking, the word baptize means to "wash with water," whether by immersing, pouring, splashing or sprinkling. … the manner in which the water is applied or the amount of water applied is not a matter of central importance for the practice of Baptism and has no impact on its blessings and benefits.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “How Is the Water to Be Applied in Baptism?”

Confirmation

In short summary, not a quoteAt confirmation, people baptized as infants publicly confess the faith their parents and sponsors confessed for them.

“children who are confirmed have opportunity to confess publicly with their own mouths the faith in which they have been baptized and instructed, just as their parents and sponsors had confessed for them when they were infants”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “How Is Teaching Carried Out in Connection with Baptism?”

Likely questions

  1. Why do Lutherans baptize infants?

    In short summary, not a quoteChrist commanded baptizing all nations, and that includes infants. Children belong to Christ's promised redemption, just as God brought infants into His covenant through circumcision.

    “Therefore, it is necessary to baptize little children, that the promise of salvation may be applied to them, according to Christ’s command to baptize all nations (Matthew 28:19). Just as in this passage salvation is offered to all, so Baptism is offered to all, to men, women, children, infants. It clearly follows, therefore, that infants are to be baptized, because salvation is offered with Baptism.”

    Apology of the Augsburg Confession, Art. IX, ¶52

    “Jesus Himself has commanded: "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them … [and] teaching them" (Matt. 28:19–20) — an all-encompassing mandate which excludes no age group.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “What are the biblical and theological reasons for baptizing infants?”

    “Of the Baptism of children, we hold that children should be baptized, for they belong to the promised redemption made through Christ [Acts 2:39]. Therefore, the Church should administer Baptism to them.”

    Smalcald Articles, Part III, Art. V, ¶4

    “God provided for the incorporation of such offspring, even as infants, into the identity of God's covenant people through the sign of circumcision on the eighth day (Gen 17:9–11).”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “We Are Made Children of Abraham through Baptism”
    NotesExplanation and background

    Baptism is God's gift, and Peter says the promise is for you and your children Acts 2:39. Infants are included in "all nations" Matthew 28:19.

    What "all nations" means. The Greek is πάντα τὰ ἔθνη (panta ta ethnē). Ethnos doesn't mean a nation in the modern, political sense. It means a people: a group bound together by family, language, and customs. In the plural it often means "the Gentiles," the non-Jewish peoples, and scholars debate whether Jesus means all the Gentiles or all peoples, Israel included. Either way, He is speaking of whole peoples, made up of families of every age, and He gives no age limit Matthew 28:19-20.

    Who "them" is. In "baptizing them" (βαπτίζοντες αὐτούς, baptizontes autous), the word autous is masculine, while ethnē is neuter. Greek often shifts like this when a pronoun points to the people within a group. Jesus isn't sending the apostles to baptize nations as institutions, but the people who make them up.

    How the apostles would have heard it. For about 2,000 years, God brought infants into His covenant at eight days old Genesis 17:12. When Israel entered the covenant, the little ones stood alongside the adults Deuteronomy 29:10-12. Jewish apostles told to baptize the nations would assume children were included unless Jesus said otherwise, and He never did.

    How disciples are made. In Greek the one command is "make disciples." "Baptizing" and "teaching" both describe how that happens. With adult converts, teaching usually comes first. With children of believers, Lutherans baptize first and teach as the child grows.

    The promise to children. Right after telling people to be baptized, Peter says the promise is for them and their children Acts 2:39. The word he uses, teknon, means offspring. His Jewish listeners knew God's promise to Abraham to be God to him and his offspring Genesis 17:7. "All who are far off" extends the same promise to the Gentiles Ephesians 2:13.

    The honest limit. No single verse says "baptize infants." The case rests on several lines that point the same way: God's covenant pattern, Christ's command to all nations, the promise to children, whole households baptized, and the early church's practice. Origen wrote that the church received infant baptism from the apostles, and a council of 66 bishops around AD 253 treated it as normal.

    In the Hebrew. God's promise in Genesis 17:7 is to Abraham's זַרְעֲךָ (zarʿakha), "your offspring" or "seed," which included infants from eight days old Genesis 17:12.

  2. What does baptism do?

    In short summary, not a quoteBaptism saves. Through it God forgives sins, rescues from death and the devil, gives the Holy Spirit, and makes us members of Christ's church. Faith receives these gifts.

    “It works forgiveness of sins, delivers from death and the devil, and gives eternal salvation to all who believe this, as the words and promises of God declare.”

    Small Catechism, Baptism

    “the power, work, profit, fruit, and purpose of Baptism is this—to save [1 Peter 3:21]. … We know that to be saved is nothing other than to be delivered from sin, death, and the devil [Colossians 1:13–14]. It means to enter into Christ’s kingdom [John 3:5], and to live with Him forever.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶24–25

    “Not only are our sins forgiven (Acts 2:38), but we receive the Holy Spirit (Acts 2:39), and we are made members of His Body, the Church (1 Cor. 12:13).”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “Why does an adult need to be baptized if he or she is already a believer?”
    NotesExplanation and background

    God washes away sin and gives new birth and the Holy Spirit Acts 2:38, Acts 22:16, Titus 3:5. Peter says plainly that baptism now saves you 1 Peter 3:21.

    What Scripture says baptism gives. Forgiveness and the Holy Spirit Acts 2:38. Washing away of sins Acts 22:16. New birth Titus 3:5, John 3:5. Union with Christ's death and resurrection Romans 6:3-4. Being clothed with Christ Galatians 3:27. Membership in His body 1 Corinthians 12:13. Salvation 1 Peter 3:21.

    Where the power comes from. Not from the water. The Small Catechism says it is God's Word in and with the water, received by faith. Without the Word it is plain water.

    Its effect lasts a lifetime. Baptism happens once, but Luther taught that it shapes the whole Christian life. Each day we return to it in repentance, the old sinful self is drowned, and a new person rises to live before God Romans 6:3-4.

  3. Isn't that salvation by works?

    In short summary, not a quoteBaptism is not our work. It is God's work and His gift, which faith receives. Treating it as our promise to God turns grace into law.

    “But if the “new spirits” say, as they are accustomed, “Still Baptism is itself a work, and you say works are of no use for salvation. What, then, becomes of faith?” Answer, “Yes, our works, indeed, do nothing for salvation. Baptism, however, is not our work, but God’s.””

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶35

    “So you see plainly that there is no work done here by us, but a treasure, which God gives us and faith grasps [Ephesians 2:8–9].”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶37

    “Is Baptism primarily God's promise to us or is Baptism primarily our promise to God? … such a view of Baptism fundamentally alters its character from that of grace and gift (Gospel) to that of human assent, effort and responsibility (Law). This makes all the difference!”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “What are the biblical and theological reasons for baptizing infants?”
    NotesExplanation and background

    No. A baby does nothing. God saved us not by works done by us, but by His mercy and the washing of regeneration Titus 3:5. Baptism is grace received, not achieved.

    What Paul means by works. A work is something we do to earn righteousness, like wages earned for labor Romans 4:4-5. The false teachers in Galatia made circumcision a requirement of the law for being justified, and Paul says that cuts a person off from grace Galatians 5:2-4.

    Who acts in baptism. In Titus 3:5, God is the one doing the saving, and He saves through the washing. Paul sets that directly against works done by us Titus 3:5. He connects the "circumcision made without hands" with being buried with Christ in baptism Colossians 2:11-12.

    Faith is no different. Faith is also something that happens in us, yet Scripture doesn't count it as a work that earns anything, because God gives it Ephesians 2:8-9. Lutherans see baptism the same way. An infant baptism shows this most clearly: the baby brings nothing and receives everything.

  4. Can a baby really have faith?

    In short summary, not a quoteYes. Infant faith is trust in God, not intellectual understanding. Lutherans baptize because God commands it, and they pray for the child's faith.

    “To maintain that infants are given faith in Baptism does not imply that they intellectually grasp all that this entails (knowledge) or volitionally acknowledge it as true (assent). But they trust the one who holds them. And as they grow, they gradually become more aware and mindful of all that comes with the promises of God.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “Are infants or people who have mental or emotional limitations capable of believing the promises of Baptism?”

    “faith is an orientation toward God whereby we receive and live from the gifts of God — whether we are conscious of them or not”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “Are infants or people who have mental or emotional limitations capable of believing the promises of Baptism?”

    “We bring the child in the conviction and hope that it believes, and we pray that God may grant it faith [Luke 17:2; Ephesians 2:8]. But we do not baptize it for that reason, but solely because of God’s command.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶57
    NotesExplanation and background

    Faith is God's gift, not intellectual understanding Ephesians 2:8-9. Jesus speaks of little ones who believe in Him Matthew 18:6, and David says God made him trust at his mother's breasts Psalm 22:9.

    Faith is trust, not a test of knowledge. Even in adults, faith is created by the Holy Spirit, not by intellect 1 Corinthians 12:3. Knowledge grows over a lifetime, but trust can be present from the start.

    Scripture shows faith in the very young. David says God made him trust from his mother's breasts Psalm 22:9. John the Baptist was filled with the Spirit from the womb Luke 1:15 and leaped for joy at Jesus' presence before birth Luke 1:41-44. Timothy knew the Scriptures from infancy 2 Timothy 3:15.

    In the Greek. Luke calls John the Baptist τὸ βρέφος (to brephos), "the baby," while he was still in the womb Luke 1:41-44. Paul says Timothy knew the Scriptures ἀπὸ βρέφους (apo brephous), "from infancy" 2 Timothy 3:15.

    Jesus makes the child the model. He doesn't say children must become like adults to enter the kingdom. He says adults must receive it like a child Luke 18:15-17, Mark 10:13-16.

    What the LCMS report says. Asked whether infants and people with mental limitations can believe, the LCMS report answers "Yes." It describes faith as trusting dependence on God, whether or not a person is conscious of His gifts.

  5. Why do infants need baptism?

    In short summary, not a quoteEveryone since Adam is born sinful, and that sin condemns those who are not born again through baptism and the Holy Spirit.

    “since the fall of Adam [Romans 5:12], all who are naturally born are born with sin [Psalm 51:5], that is, without the fear of God, without trust in God, and with the inclination to sin, called concupiscence. Concupiscence is a disease and original vice that is truly sin. It damns and brings eternal death on those who are not born anew through Baptism and the Holy Spirit [John 3:5].”

    Augsburg Confession, Art. II, ¶1–2

    “The Word of God also teaches that we are all conceived and born sinful and are under the power of the devil until Christ claims us as His own. We would be lost forever unless delivered from sin, death, and everlasting condemnation”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), quoting the LSB Rite of Holy Baptism
    NotesExplanation and background

    We are sinful from conception Psalm 51:5, and what is born of the flesh is flesh John 3:6, even from Christian parents.

    Sin is a condition, not only an act. David says he was sinful from conception Psalm 51:5. Paul says sin and death spread to all people through Adam Romans 5:12, and that we were by nature children of wrath Ephesians 2:3.

    In the Hebrew. Psalm 51:5 (verse 7 in Hebrew Bibles) begins הֵן־בְּעָווֹן חוֹלָלְתִּי (hen-beʿawon cholalti), "in iniquity I was brought forth." David traces his sin to his birth and conception, not to his first wrong act.

    Death reigns over infants too. Paul says death reigned even over those who had not sinned the way Adam did Romans 5:14. Infants die, and Scripture treats death as the result of sin.

    So infants need rebirth. What is born of the flesh is flesh, and everyone needs to be born of water and the Spirit John 3:5-6, John 3:7. Christian parents pass on human nature, not new birth John 1:12-13. Baptizing infants isn't about fear. It is giving them the gift God offers.

  6. Does it matter if the person was too young to understand?

    In short summary, not a quoteA baptism's validity depends on God's Word, not on what the person understood. Like an adopted infant, the child receives God's promise before being able to agree to it. Confirmation is the later public confession.

    “we are not very concerned to know whether the person baptized believes or not. For Baptism does not become invalid on that account. But everything depends on God’s Word and command. … For my faith does not make Baptism, but receives it.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶52–53

    “It is analogous to parents who adopt an infant child. They promise to care for the child apart from that child's ability to assent.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “What are the biblical and theological reasons for baptizing infants?”

    “Nevertheless, I am baptized. And if I am baptized, it is promised to me that I shall be saved and have eternal life, both in soul and body.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶44

    “children who are confirmed have opportunity to confess publicly with their own mouths the faith in which they have been baptized and instructed”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “How Is Teaching Carried Out in Connection with Baptism?”
    NotesExplanation and background

    No. A baptism rests on what God did and promised, not on what the person understood. People baptized as infants publicly confess that faith later, at confirmation.

    Validity rests on God, not on what the person knew. The Large Catechism teaches that faith does not make baptism; it receives it. Baptism is valid because God commanded it and attached His promise to it.

    A comparison. A child can be given an inheritance or citizenship long before understanding it, and the gift is still real. Understanding grows into what was already given, as with Timothy, who was taught the Scriptures from infancy 2 Timothy 3:15.

    The public confession comes later. At confirmation, after instruction, people baptized as infants confess this faith before the congregation. That is the personal declaration Baptists value. Lutherans simply believe God's gift comes first, and our confession answers it.

  7. What about people baptized as babies who never believe?

    In short summary, not a quoteBaptism's promise stays in place, and anyone who falls away can return to it. Without faith it brings no benefit, so parents and sponsors help the child keep the faith.

    “Our Baptism abides forever. Even though someone should fall from Baptism and sin, still we always have access to it. So we may subdue the old man again.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶77

    “Without faith it profits nothing, even though Baptism is in itself a divine overwhelming treasure.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶34

    “They are to support and help the parents nurture the child as he or she grows so that the child may grasp and hold firmly to the promises of God throughout his or her life.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), on sponsors
    NotesExplanation and background

    Faith must be nurtured, and people can fall away. The same is true of people baptized as adults.

    Baptism isn't a guarantee apart from faith. The LCMS teaches that faith can be lost. Paul warns that people can fall away from grace Galatians 5:4. All Israel was baptized into Moses, yet most of them fell in the wilderness 1 Corinthians 10:1-5.

    That's why parents and sponsors make promises. At an infant baptism, parents and sponsors promise to raise the child in the faith, because faith must be fed by God's Word.

    The same is true of believer's baptism. People baptized as adults also walk away. Baptists usually say those people were never truly saved, which means a believer's baptism doesn't guarantee anything either. When someone returns to faith, Lutherans don't rebaptize them. They return to the promise God already made.

  8. What about the thief on the cross?

    In short summary, not a quoteChrist commands baptism so strictly that the Large Catechism says we must be baptized or we cannot be saved. Still, the LCMS report says that when a baby of Christian parents who wanted the child baptized dies first, the lack of baptism does not by itself mean the child is lost.

    “Furthermore, Baptism is most solemnly and strictly commanded so that we must be baptized or we cannot be saved.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶6

    “This loving God did not institute Baptism as something one has to do to be saved (a work of the Law); rather, He gave Baptism as a means of salvation (a gift of the Gospel). … Yet the absence of Baptism, in itself, does not cause someone to be lost.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), on infants who die unbaptized

    These sources don't discuss the thief on the cross. The closest statements are above. The second one comes from a passage about infants of Christian parents who die before baptism, and the report limits it to parents who wanted their child baptized and did not despise baptism.

    NotesExplanation and background

    God can save without baptism Luke 23:43. He isn't bound by it, but we are bound to it, because He commanded it Matthew 28:19.

    God bound us, not Himself. Lutherans teach that God has bound us to baptism, because Christ commanded it Matthew 28:19, but He is not bound by it. He can save without it, as He did the thief Luke 23:43.

    The thief's situation was unique. He died before Christ's resurrection and before Jesus gave the command to baptize. He had no opportunity to be baptized. An exception like that doesn't cancel the normal promise.

    Lacking baptism differs from refusing it. Jesus' words connect condemnation to unbelief, not to missing baptism Mark 16:16. Note that some of the earliest manuscripts do not include Mark 16:9-20, and both the ESV and NASB mark it, so don't rest the point on this verse alone.

  9. Should anyone be rebaptized?

    In short summary, not a quoteNo. Infant baptisms are valid, should never be repeated, and there is only one baptism.

    “As we have said, even though infants did not believe (which, however, is not the case), still their Baptism would be valid. We have now shown this. No one should rebaptize infants.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶55

    “Even if we were put under the water a hundred times, it would still be only one Baptism, even though the work and sign continue and remain.”

    Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶78

    “"One Baptism" highlights the dramatic once-and-for-all nature of Baptism that transitions us to a totally new and wondrous life and future.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “Baptism Promises the Forgiveness of Sins”
    NotesExplanation and background

    No. There is one baptism Ephesians 4:5. Rebaptizing would treat God's first promise as if it never happened.

    One baptism. Paul lists one baptism alongside one Lord and one faith Ephesians 4:5. Baptism is God's act, and God doesn't need to do it twice.

    What rebaptism would imply. Repeating a baptism would say either that God's first promise failed, or that it was only water. Both contradict what Lutherans believe baptism is.

    The church came to agree. The Council of Arles (AD 314) and Augustine, writing against the Donatists, who rebaptized Christians, taught that a baptism with water in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit is not repeated. Earlier, Cyprian's North African church had rebaptized converts from heretical groups, so this took time to settle.

    If someone doubts their baptism. If the doubt is whether it "worked," the answer is to return to it in faith, not to repeat it. If there is real doubt whether a baptism with water in the triune name happened at all, a pastor may baptize conditionally, which is not rebaptism.

  10. Does the LCMS accept baptisms from other churches?

    In short summary, not a quoteYes, from churches that confess the Trinity. A baptism with water in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit is valid, whatever the person's age and however the water was applied.

    “The application of water is a Christian Baptism when it is performed in the name of the Father, Son and Holy Spirit as Jesus instructed.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “What Is the Significance of God’s Name in Baptism?”

    “faith does not make this particular washing with water a Baptism. Only God's name and promise do that. Faith may precede Baptism (as with adult converts); faith may be created in and through Baptism; faith may be sustained through the promise after Baptism.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “What Is the Purpose of God’s Promises to Us in Baptism?”

    “the manner in which the water is applied or the amount of water applied is not a matter of central importance for the practice of Baptism and has no impact on its blessings and benefits.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “How Is the Water to Be Applied in Baptism?”
    NotesExplanation and background

    Yes. The LCMS accepts a baptism with water in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit from churches that confess the Trinity, Baptist churches included.

    What the LCMS recognizes. A baptism with water in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit from a church that confesses the Trinity, Baptist churches included, whatever the person's age and whether it was done by immersion, pouring, or sprinkling. Baptisms from groups that deny the Trinity, such as Latter-day Saints, are not accepted. A pastor can help when there's a question.

    The word itself. βαπτίζω (baptizō) can mean simply to wash. Mark 7:4 speaks of βαπτισμοὺς ποτηρίων (baptismous potēriōn), the "washing of cups," and in Luke 11:38 a Pharisee is surprised that Jesus did not first wash (ἐβαπτίσθη, ebaptisthē) before dinner Mark 7:4, Luke 11:38. The word doesn't require immersion.

    Why a Baptist baptism counts. In the Lutheran view, a baptism is valid because God's Word was joined to the water, not because of the person's decision. So Lutherans believe God truly gave His gifts in that baptism, even if the church that performed it describes it differently.

  11. When should children be baptized?

    In short summary, not a quoteThe LCMS encourages baptizing babies as soon as possible, because God's promises are for children too.

    “This is why Christian parents bring their babies for Baptism as soon as possible: because of the clear promises of God even for children (Acts 2:39), who certainly also need a Savior from sin and death (Rom. 6:23).”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “What if the baby of Christian parents dies before it is baptized?”

    “As we were brought to Jesus in Baptism, we now bring our infants, children and families to Jesus that they might also become His followers and citizens in God's realm.”

    LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “Who Is to Be Baptized?”
    NotesExplanation and background

    The LCMS encourages baptizing children as infants, as soon as possible after birth.

    What the LCMS encourages. Baptizing children as infants, soon after birth, with parents and sponsors promising to raise them in the faith.

    Why not wait. The promise is for children too Acts 2:39, and they need a Savior as much as adults do Romans 6:23.

    Baptism or dedication. A dedication is the parents' promise to God. Lutherans see baptism as God's promise to the child, which is why they baptize instead of dedicating.

Common objections

Objection · Baptists and many evangelicalsBaptism is an outward sign of a decision already made.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteThe Confessions teach that sacraments are not mainly our public declaration. They are God's work and promise toward us.

“Our churches teach that the Sacraments were ordained, not only to be marks of profession among men, but even more, to be signs and testimonies of God’s will toward us.”

Augsburg Confession, Art. XIII, ¶1

“the world is now so full of sects arguing that Baptism is an outward thing and that outward things are of no benefit. But let Baptism be a thoroughly outward thing. Here stand God’s Word and command, which institute, establish, and confirm Baptism.”

Large Catechism, Baptism, ¶7–8

“This mistake can happen even with regard to our perception of adult Baptism, if we see Baptism as little more than the outward response to an inward reality.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), section “Are infants or people who have mental or emotional limitations capable of believing the promises of Baptism?”
NotesExplanation and background

Scripture describes what God does in baptism: forgiveness, washing, the Spirit, salvation Acts 2:38, Acts 22:16, Titus 3:5, 1 Peter 3:21. Baptists often point to 1 Peter 3:21 ("pledge of a good conscience" in some translations), but the same verse says baptism "now saves you."

Where the Baptist view comes from. Baptists want to protect salvation by faith alone Romans 10:9, and they worry that saying baptism saves turns it into a work. Lutherans share that concern.

What the passages say. Where the New Testament says what baptism does, it names a gift from God: forgiveness, washing, new birth, the Spirit, salvation. Baptists point to 1 Peter 3:21, which some translations render "the pledge of a good conscience toward God." Lutherans read it as an "appeal to God" and note that the same verse says baptism "now saves you" 1 Peter 3:20-21.

Where the idea took shape. The view of baptism mainly as our pledge took shape during the Reformation. Zwingli called sacraments pledges of allegiance, though he still baptized infants. The Anabaptists, and later Baptists, applied the idea to baptize only professing believers.

Faith and baptism don't compete. Romans 10:9 and Acts 2:38 describe the same salvation. Faith receives what God gives in baptism.

Where Lutherans and Baptists agree

A summary, not an LCMS source. The two traditions agree on far more than they disagree on. The difference is how God works in baptism, not whether He saves.

  • Only Jesus can wash away sins.
  • Salvation is by grace through faith, not by works Ephesians 2:8-9.
  • Christ commanded baptism, and it should never be treated lightly Matthew 28:19.
  • Baptism is done with water in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
  • Both commend little ones who die to God's mercy, Baptists usually with more certainty than the LCMS, which speaks of hope for children of Christian parents.
  • Both want children raised to know the Lord.

What the LCMS teaches about the Lord's Supper

In the words of the Lutheran Confessions and the LCMS.

What the Lord's Supper is

In short summary, not a quoteIt is Christ's true body and blood, in and under the bread and wine, given for Christians to eat and drink.

“It is the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, in and under the bread and wine, which we Christians are commanded by Christ’s Word to eat and to drink.”

Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶8

What it gives

In short summary, not a quoteForgiveness of sins, and with it life and salvation.

“For this reason we go to the Sacrament: there we receive such a treasure by and in which we gain forgiveness of sins.”

Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶22

“For where there is forgiveness of sins, there is also life and salvation.”

Small Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar

The Word makes the Sacrament

In short summary, not a quoteChrist's Word joined to the bread and wine makes them His body and blood. Without the Word they are ordinary bread and wine.

“It is the Word, I say, that makes and sets this Sacrament apart. So it is not mere bread and wine, but is, and is called, Christ’s body and blood [1 Corinthians 11:23–27].”

Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶10

Christ's power, not the pastor's

In short summary, not a quoteNo pastor or ceremony makes Christ present. His own almighty power does, through His words.

“no work of man or recitation of the minister produces this presence of Christ’s body and blood in the Holy Supper. Instead, this presence is to be credited only and alone to the almighty power of our Lord Jesus Christ.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶8

Received with the mouth

In short summary, not a quoteChrist's body and blood are received with the bread and wine, by mouth, in a heavenly way that is beyond human understanding.

“Christ’s body and blood are received with the bread and wine, not only spiritually through faith, but also orally. Yet not in a “Capernaitic” way, but in a supernatural, heavenly way, because of the sacramental union.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶15

Faith receives the blessing

In short summary, not a quoteWhoever believes Christ's words "for you" and "for the forgiveness of sins" is ready to receive it.

“But a person is truly worthy and well prepared who has faith in these words, “Given … and shed for you for the forgiveness of sins.””

Small Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar

Unbelievers receive it to judgment

In short summary, not a quoteChrist's body and blood are present for everyone who eats and drinks. Those who believe receive them for life, and those who don't, for judgment.

“We believe, teach, and confess that not only the true believers in Christ and the worthy, but also the unworthy and unbelievers receive Christ’s true body and blood. However, they do not receive them for life and consolation, but for judgment and condemnation, if they are not converted and do not repent (1 Corinthians 11:27–29).”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶16

Both bread and wine

In short summary, not a quoteChrist commanded that all drink from the cup, so the cup is given to everyone, not only to clergy.

“The laity are given both kinds in the Sacrament of the Lord’s Supper because this practice has the Lord’s command, “Drink of it, all of you” (Matthew 26:27). Christ has clearly commanded that all should drink from the cup.”

In short summary, not a quoteChrist's death on the cross is the one sacrifice for sins. The Supper gives its benefits. It doesn't repeat it.

A gift and a sign of unity

In short summary, not a quoteThe Supper unites us with God through forgiveness and with one another as one body.

Likely questions

  1. What is the Lord's Supper?

    In short summary, not a quoteIt is the true body and blood of Jesus Christ, in and under the bread and wine, given by Christ for Christians to eat and drink.

    “It is the true body and blood of our Lord Jesus Christ, under the bread and wine, for us Christians to eat and to drink, instituted by Christ Himself.”

    Small Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar

    “Our churches teach that the body and blood of Christ are truly present and distributed to those who eat the Lord’s Supper [1 Corinthians 10:16]. They reject those who teach otherwise.”

    Augsburg Confession, Art. X, ¶1–2
    NotesExplanation and background

    Many names, one meal. Lutherans call it the Lord's Supper 1 Corinthians 11:20-22, Holy Communion 1 Corinthians 10:16-17, the Sacrament of the Altar, and the Eucharist (from the Greek for "giving thanks").

    Instituted by Christ. Jesus gave it on the night He was betrayed Matthew 26:26-28, Luke 22:19-20, and Paul received the same words from the Lord 1 Corinthians 11:23-26.

  2. Is Christ's body really present, or is it a symbol?

    In short summary, not a quoteReally present. Christ's words "This is My body" mean what they say. The bread is a true participation in His body, not a reminder of an absent one.

    “We believe, teach, and confess that in the Holy Supper Christ’s body and blood are truly and essentially present, and that they are truly distributed and received with the bread and wine.”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶6

    “We believe, teach, and confess that the words of Christ’s testament are not to be understood in any other way than the way they read, according to the letter. So the bread does not signify Christ’s absent body and the wine His absent blood.”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶7

    “Since Paul says, “The bread that we break, is it not a participation in the body of Christ?” (1 Corinthians 10:16), it would follow that if the Lord’s body were not truly present, the bread is not a communion of the body, but only of Christ’s spirit.”

    Apology of the Augsburg Confession, Art. X, ¶54
    NotesExplanation and background

    Paul's question expects "yes." He asks whether the bread is a participation in Christ's body 1 Corinthians 10:16-17, and he warns that eating unworthily makes a person guilty of the body and blood of the Lord 1 Corinthians 11:27-29. That warning is hard to explain if only bread is present.

    A dying man's testament. Jesus spoke these words the night before His death. Lutherans read them the way a last will is read: plainly, as the speaker meant them.

  3. Does the bread stop being bread?

    In short summary, not a quoteNo. Lutherans reject transubstantiation. The bread remains bread and the wine remains wine, and together with them Christ's body and blood are truly present.

    “As for transubstantiation, we care nothing about the sophistic cunning by which they teach that bread and wine leave or lose their own natural substance so that only the appearance and color of bread remain, and not true bread. For it is in perfect agreement with Holy Scriptures that there is, and remains, bread, as Paul himself calls it, “The bread that we break” [1 Corinthians 10:16] and “Let a person … so eat of the bread” [1 Corinthians 11:28].”

    Smalcald Articles, Part III, Art. VI, ¶5
    NotesExplanation and background

    Paul still calls it bread. Even after the blessing, Paul speaks of "the bread that we break" 1 Corinthians 10:16-17 and says a person should examine himself and so eat "of the bread" 1 Corinthians 11:27-29.

    How Lutherans describe it. Lutherans speak of a "sacramental union": Christ's body and blood are present in, with, and under the bread and wine. They don't try to explain how, beyond Christ's own words.

    Not "consubstantiation." Lutherans are often said to teach consubstantiation, the idea that two substances exist side by side. The LCMS rejects the label because it suggests a theory of how Christ is present, such as a natural mixing or His body enclosed in the bread. It confesses only what Christ's words say: the bread is His body and the cup is His blood.

  4. What does the Lord's Supper give?

    In short summary, not a quoteForgiveness of sins, and with forgiveness, life and salvation. It also strengthens faith for the Christian life.

    “Now examine further the effectiveness and benefits that really caused the Sacrament to be instituted. This is its most necessary part, so that we may know what we should seek and gain there. … For this reason we go to the Sacrament: there we receive such a treasure by and in which we gain forgiveness of sins.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶20–22

    “For where there is forgiveness of sins, there is also life and salvation.”

    Small Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar

    “On this account it is indeed called a food of souls, which nourishes and strengthens the new man. … Therefore, the Sacrament is given as a daily pasture and sustenance, that faith may refresh and strengthen itself [Psalm 23:1–3] so that it will not fall back in such a battle, but become ever stronger and stronger.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶23–24
    NotesExplanation and background

    The words say it. Jesus said His blood is "poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins" Matthew 26:26-28. Lutherans take the Supper's benefit directly from those words.

    Food for the journey. Baptism begins the Christian life. The Supper sustains it, as food sustains the body.

  5. How can eating and drinking do such great things?

    In short summary, not a quoteThe eating and drinking don't do it by themselves. Christ's words, as the Small Catechism gives them, "given and shed for you for the forgiveness of sins" do, and faith believes them.

    “It is not the eating and drinking, indeed, that does them, but the words, which are given here, “Given … and shed for you, for the forgiveness of sins.” These words are, beside the bodily eating and drinking, the chief thing in the Sacrament. The person who believes these words has what they say and express, namely, the forgiveness of sins.”

    Small Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar

    “Although the work is done and the forgiveness of sins is secured by the cross [John 19:30], it cannot come to us in any other way than through the Word.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶31
    NotesExplanation and background

    Won on the cross, delivered in the Supper. Lutherans distinguish where forgiveness was won (once, at the cross Hebrews 10:10-14) from how it is handed out (through the Word, Baptism, and the Supper).

  6. What makes it Christ's body: the pastor, or our faith?

    In short summary, not a quoteNeither. Christ's own word and power make His body and blood present. A pastor's unworthiness can't undo it, and our faith doesn't create it. Faith receives it.

    “no work of man or recitation of the minister produces this presence of Christ’s body and blood in the Holy Supper. Instead, this presence is to be credited only and alone to the almighty power of our Lord Jesus Christ.”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶8

    “For the Sacrament is not founded upon people’s holiness, but upon God’s Word.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶16

    “Both the Sacraments and Word are effective because of Christ’s institution and command, even if they are administered by evil men.”

    Augsburg Confession, Art. VIII, ¶2
    NotesExplanation and background

    Why this matters. If the Supper depended on the pastor's holiness or on the strength of our faith, no one could be sure of it. Resting it on Christ's word makes it certain.

  7. Who receives it worthily?

    In short summary, not a quoteAnyone who believes Christ's words "for you" and "for the forgiveness of sins." Worthiness rests entirely on Christ, not on our own goodness or preparation. LCMS congregations also ask that communicants share their confession; see "Why do LCMS churches practice close communion?"

    “Fasting and bodily preparation are, indeed, fine outward training. But a person is truly worthy and well prepared who has faith in these words, “Given … and shed for you for the forgiveness of sins.” But anyone who does not believe these words, or doubts, is unworthy and unfit. For the words “for you” require hearts that truly believe.”

    Small Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar

    “We believe, teach, and confess that all the worthiness of guests of this heavenly feast is and is founded on Christ’s most holy obedience and perfect merit alone. We receive these for ourselves by true faith, and by the Sacrament we are assured of them.”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶20

    “Repentance, faith in Christ's words in and about the Sacrament, and the desire for repentant living in unity with one's fellow communicant are the components of communing in a worthy fashion.”

    LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999), Appendix, Question 7
    NotesExplanation and background

    Self-examination. Paul tells each person to examine himself before eating and drinking 1 Corinthians 11:27-29. In Lutheran practice that means repenting of sin, trusting Christ's promise, and recognizing His body and blood in the Supper.

  8. What if I feel too unworthy to come?

    In short summary, not a quoteThat is exactly who the Supper is for. It was given for Christians who are weak in faith but repentant. Among those who share this faith, only those who don't want forgiveness or don't intend to turn from sin should stay away.

    “We believe, teach, and confess that no true believer—as long as he has living faith, however weak he may be—receives the Holy Supper to his judgment. For the Supper was instituted especially for Christians weak in faith, yet repentant. It was instituted for their consolation and to strengthen their weak faith [Matthew 9:12; 11:5, 28].”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶19

    “We are not baptized because we are worthy and holy. Nor do we go to Confession because we are pure and without sin. On the contrary, we go because we are poor, miserable people. We go exactly because we are unworthy.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶61

    “If, therefore, you are heavy laden and feel your weakness, then go joyfully to this Sacrament and receive refreshment, comfort, and strength [Matthew 11:28].”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶72
    NotesExplanation and background

    An invitation, not a test. Luther pointed to Jesus' own words: "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden" Matthew 11:28.

  9. Do unbelievers receive Christ's body too?

    In short summary, not a quoteYes. Christ's body and blood are present whether or not a person believes. Believers receive them for forgiveness and life. Unbelievers who don't repent receive them to their judgment.

    “Of the Sacrament of the Altar, we hold that the bread and wine in the Supper are Christ’s true body and blood. These are given and received not only by the godly but also by wicked Christians [1 Corinthians 11:29–30].”

    Smalcald Articles, Part III, Art. VI, ¶1

    “We believe, teach, and confess that not only the true believers in Christ and the worthy, but also the unworthy and unbelievers receive Christ’s true body and blood. However, they do not receive them for life and consolation, but for judgment and condemnation, if they are not converted and do not repent (1 Corinthians 11:27–29).”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶16
    NotesExplanation and background

    Why this is serious. Paul says those who eat and drink without discerning the body eat and drink judgment on themselves 1 Corinthians 11:27-29. That is one reason Lutheran pastors care about who comes to the altar.

  10. Why do LCMS churches practice close communion?

    In short summary, not a quoteFor two reasons: to protect people from receiving the Supper without faith in what it is, and because communing together is a sign of shared faith and confession. It is not a judgment that visitors aren't Christians.

    “There are two reasons why people can be refused admission to the Lord's Supper. The first has to do with faith and discerning the body. … But the second reason has to do with the need for a fitting confessional unity among those who commune together.”

    LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999), Appendix, Question 1

    “the "genuine faith" required for receiving the Eucharist worthily includes a proper understanding of and faith in what the Sacrament is, that is, in the doctrine of the real presence of Christ's body and blood in and under the sacramental elements of bread and wine.”

    LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999)

    “The body of the Lord is not usually given to those who have not been examined [1 Corinthians 11:27–28] and absolved.”

    Augsburg Confession, Art. XXV, ¶1
    NotesExplanation and background

    Close or closed. The LCMS uses both terms for the same practice. "Close" highlights communion among those who share one confession.

    Unity in the act itself. Paul says that because there is one bread, we who are many are one body 1 Corinthians 10:16-17. Communing together publicly declares that unity.

  11. Isn't close communion unloving?

    In short summary, not a quoteThe LCMS practices it out of love and concern, with sorrow over the divisions among Christians. It doesn't assume visitors lack faith, and pastors may make exceptions in times of crisis or personal spiritual need.

    “when we explain, with genuine interest and friendliness, our doctrine that the Lord's Supper is both a gift and a sign of unity, others will come to see that we do what we do not because we are unfriendly but because of what we firmly believe.”

    LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999), Appendix, Question 2

    “Our practice of not communing those who belong to such church bodies does not mean that we fear that such individual Christians would necessarily commune in an unworthy manner.”

    LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999)

    “We do this not with any thought of superiority or with any gladness, but in deep sorrow and prayer to the Lord of the Church that he would remove the divisions that separate His children from one another in their visible expressions of fellowship.”

    LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999)

    “we must at all times acknowledge the possibility, even necessity, of situations of crisis or personal spiritual need in which these fellow Christians as individual believers in need of the Sacrament's comfort and strength might receive such also from our pastors and at our altars.”

    LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999)
    NotesExplanation and background

    Not unusual. The same report notes that the Roman Catholic and Orthodox churches teach something very similar. Outside the report, many Baptist churches also limit the Supper to baptized believers.

  12. How often should Christians receive it?

    In short summary, not a quoteOften. Christ's words "Do this, as often as you drink it" (1 Corinthians 11:25) imply frequent use, and the early Lutheran churches offered the Supper every Sunday and on festivals.

    “Yes, since He speaks the words ‘As often as you drink it,’ it is still implied that we should do it often.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶47

    “These are inviting and commanding words by which all who would be Christians are told to partake of this Sacrament.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶45

    “Masses are celebrated among us every Lord’s Day and on the other festivals. The Sacrament is offered to those who wish to use it, after they have been examined and absolved.”

    Apology of the Augsburg Confession, Art. XXIV, ¶1
    NotesExplanation and background

    Today. Many LCMS congregations offer the Supper every Sunday. Some offer it every other week or at certain services.

  13. Why do we receive both the bread and the cup?

    In short summary, not a quoteBecause Christ commanded all to drink. Giving only the bread changes what He instituted.

    “The laity are given both kinds in the Sacrament of the Lord’s Supper because this practice has the Lord’s command, “Drink of it, all of you” (Matthew 26:27). Christ has clearly commanded that all should drink from the cup.”

    Augsburg Confession, Art. XXII, ¶1–2

    “We do not need that “high reasoning” that teaches there is as much under the one kind as under both, as the sophists and the Council of Constance teach. Even if that were true, giving the one kind only is not the entire ordinance and institution commanded by Christ [Galatians 1:9].”

    Smalcald Articles, Part III, Art. VI, ¶2–3
    NotesExplanation and background

    Mark's account agrees. "And they all drank of it" Mark 14:22-24.

  14. Is the Lord's Supper a sacrifice?

    In short summary, not a quoteNot a sacrifice for sins. Christ's death on the cross was the one, complete sacrifice. Lutherans kept the Mass as a service but rejected the idea that it is offered to God for the sins of the living and the dead.

    “Our churches are falsely accused of abolishing the Mass. The Mass is held among us and celebrated with the highest reverence.”

    Augsburg Confession, Art. XXIV, ¶1

    “This means that Christ is the only sacrifice applied on behalf of the sins of others. Therefore, in the New Testament, no sacrifice is left to be applied for the sins of others, except the one sacrifice of Christ upon the cross.”

    Apology of the Augsburg Confession, Art. XXIV, ¶56
    A false teaching the Confessions reject

    “The papistic sacrifice of the Mass for the sins of the living and the dead.”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶23, an error it rejects
    NotesExplanation and background

    Once for all. Hebrews says Christ offered Himself once for all, and by that single offering He has perfected those who are being sanctified Hebrews 10:10-14.

  15. When may children receive the Lord's Supper?

    In short summary, not a quoteAfter instruction, when they can examine themselves and believe Christ's words. The Confessions don't set an age.

    “For it is not our intention to let people come to the Sacrament and administer it to them if they do not know what they seek or why they come.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶2

    “Since the children are baptized and received into the Christian Church, they should also enjoy this communion of the Sacrament, in order that they may serve us and be useful to us.”

    Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶87

    These sources don't name an age. Most LCMS congregations give first Communion at or near confirmation, and some admit younger children earlier after instruction and examination by the pastor. The LCMS does not commune infants, because Paul asks communicants to examine themselves.

    NotesExplanation and background

    Why instruction first. Paul calls for self-examination and discerning the body 1 Corinthians 11:27-29, so Lutherans teach children what the Supper is before they receive it.

Common objections

Each objection is stated the way its holders would put it, followed by the Lutheran answer.

Objection · Many Baptists and other evangelicals"Do this in remembrance of me" means the Supper is only a memorial.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteLutherans agree the Supper is a remembrance, but not only that. Christ commands us to remember Him by eating and drinking His true body and blood. The Confessions reject calling them mere memorials of an absent body.

“For Christ has not instituted it to be treated as a show. Instead, He has commanded His Christians to eat it, drink it, and remember Him by it.”

Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶42
A false teaching the Confessions reject

“Or, they are no more than tokens or memorials of Christ’s absent body.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. VII, ¶116, an error it rejects
NotesExplanation and background

Remembering in the biblical sense. Remembrance in Scripture is more than recalling an idea. At the Passover, Israel "remembered" by sharing the meal God gave. Jesus instituted the Supper at Passover Matthew 26:26-29.

The same verse says "This is my body." The command to remember comes right after Jesus says what He is giving Luke 22:19-20.

Objection · Many Baptists and Reformed Christians"This is my body" is a figure of speech, like "I am the door."

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteJesus' words at the Supper are to be taken as they read. They are His last will given the night before His death, not a parable or comparison.

“We believe, teach, and confess that the words of Christ’s testament are not to be understood in any other way than the way they read, according to the letter. So the bread does not signify Christ’s absent body and the wine His absent blood. But, because of the sacramental union, ‹the bread and wine› are truly Christ’s body and blood.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶7

“Here we stop to watch those who will call themselves His masters and make the matter different from what He has spoken.”

Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶13
NotesExplanation and background

Different kinds of sayings. "I am the door" John 10:9 and "I am the true vine" John 15:1 are comparisons Jesus explains in context. At the Supper He hands the disciples something and says what it is: "This is my body, which is given for you" Luke 22:19-20.

The Aramaic question. Jesus likely spoke Aramaic at the Supper, a language that often leaves out "is" ("this my body"). Some argue that leaves room for "represents." But a sentence like that still states what something is, and all four Greek accounts of the bread word write ἐστιν (estin), "is."

Paul treats it literally. He says the cup is a participation in Christ's blood 1 Corinthians 10:16-17 and that unworthy eating sins against His body and blood 1 Corinthians 11:27-29.

Objection · Reformed (Calvinist) ChristiansChrist's body is in heaven, so it can't be on the altar.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteChrist's body is not confined to one place. God's right hand, where Christ sits, is His rule over everything, and God can make Christ's body present wherever He promises.

“God’s “right hand” is everywhere. Christ is placed there in deed and in truth according to His human nature.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶12, quoting Luther

“God has and knows of various ways to be in any place, and not only one way, which philosophers call local ( localis ).”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶14, quoting Luther
A false teaching the Confessions reject

“Christ’s body is so enclosed in heaven that there is no way it can be at once and at one time in many or all places on earth where His Holy Supper is celebrated.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶32, an error it rejects
NotesExplanation and background

What "right hand" means. Paul describes Christ at God's right hand as ruling far above every power, with all things under His feet Ephesians 1:20-23. Lutherans read it as a position of power, not a seat in one location.

His promise. Jesus promised to be with His people always Matthew 28:20. Lutherans believe His word "This is my body" settles where His body is in the Supper.

Objection · Many Baptists and Reformed Christians"The flesh is no help at all" (John 6:63) shows that eating His flesh means believing.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteThe Confessions teach a spiritual eating by faith, which John 6 describes, and a separate oral eating in the Supper. They don't discuss John 6:63 directly.

“There is a twofold eating of Christ’s flesh. One is spiritual, which Christ describes especially in John 6:54. This “eating” happens in no other way than with the Spirit and faith, in preaching and meditation on the Gospel, as well as in the Lord’s Supper.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. VII, ¶61

“The other eating of Christ’s body is oral or sacramental, when Christ’s true, essential body and blood are orally received and partaken of in the Holy Supper by all who eat and drink the consecrated bread and wine in the Supper.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. VII, ¶63

These sources don't discuss John 6:63 directly.

NotesExplanation and background

A common Lutheran reading. Many Lutheran teachers note that in John 6:63 Jesus says ἡ σὰρξ οὐκ ὠφελεῖ οὐδέν (hē sarx ouk ōphelei ouden), "the flesh is no help at all": "the flesh," not "my flesh." They read it as human nature apart from the Spirit, which cannot grasp His words, not as a statement that His own flesh is useless John 6:63. That reading is a summary of Lutheran teaching, not a quote from these sources.

Objection · Reformed (Calvinist) ChristiansWe receive Christ spiritually by faith, not with our mouths.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteLutherans affirm both. Faith receives Christ spiritually, and in the Supper His body and blood are also received with the mouth, in a heavenly way.

“Christ’s body and blood are received with the bread and wine, not only spiritually through faith, but also orally. Yet not in a “Capernaitic” way, but in a supernatural, heavenly way, because of the sacramental union.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶15
A false teaching the Confessions reject

“In the Holy Supper Christ’s body is not received orally with the bread. But with the mouth only bread and wine are received. Christ’s body, however, is only received spiritually through faith.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶26, an error it rejects
NotesExplanation and background

Not crude or physical. The Confessions reject any idea that Christ's flesh is chewed or digested like ordinary food. The eating is real but supernatural, and only faith receives it for blessing.

Objection · Many BaptistsCommunion is an ordinance of obedience, not a means of grace.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteThe Supper is more than our act of obedience. Through it God gives forgiveness of sins, which faith receives.

“Our churches teach that the Sacraments were ordained, not only to be marks of profession among men, but even more, to be signs and testimonies of God’s will toward us.”

Augsburg Confession, Art. XIII, ¶1

“For this reason we go to the Sacrament: there we receive such a treasure by and in which we gain forgiveness of sins.”

Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶22
NotesExplanation and background

Obedience and gift together. Lutherans agree the Supper is commanded. But Christ's words describe what He gives: His body "given for you" Luke 22:19-20 and His blood "poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins" Matthew 26:26-28.

Objection · Many ProtestantsAnyone who loves Jesus should be welcome at the table.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteThe LCMS asks those who don't share its confession to refrain, not because it doubts their faith, but because communing together expresses shared confession, and because receiving without discerning the body brings harm.

“There are two reasons why people can be refused admission to the Lord's Supper. The first has to do with faith and discerning the body. … But the second reason has to do with the need for a fitting confessional unity among those who commune together.”

LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999), Appendix, Question 1

“When our pastors ask some to refrain from communing because they may do so "in an unworthy manner," it will be done out of love and concern for the spiritual well-being of those persons.”

LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999), Concluding Summary
NotesExplanation and background

One body. Paul ties the one bread to one body 1 Corinthians 10:16-17 and warns against divisions at the table 1 Corinthians 11:20-22.

Objection · Some ProtestantsJesus gave the Supper to Judas, so no one should be turned away.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteThe Gospels don't make it certain that Judas was present for the Supper. Even if he was, the details of that night aren't a policy to copy.

“We cannot determine with certainty on the basis of the Gospel accounts whether or not Judas was present at the institution of the Lord's Supper. But even if he were present, we cannot take all the historical details of the institution of the Eucharist as patterns that we must follow today.”

LCMS CTCR, Admission to the Lord's Supper (1999), Appendix, Question 5
NotesExplanation and background

What follows if we copy that night. The same report notes that, by that logic, only the Twelve could commune, or unrepentant people should be welcome Luke 22:3.

Objection · Roman Catholics (transubstantiation)The bread and wine become Christ's body and stop being bread and wine.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteLutherans agree Christ's body and blood are truly present, but reject the claim that the bread and wine are no longer present, only their appearance. Paul still calls it bread.

“For it is in perfect agreement with Holy Scriptures that there is, and remains, bread, as Paul himself calls it, “The bread that we break” [1 Corinthians 10:16] and “Let a person … so eat of the bread” [1 Corinthians 11:28].”

Smalcald Articles, Part III, Art. VI, ¶5
A false teaching the Confessions reject

“The papistic transubstantiation. It is taught in the papacy that during the Holy Supper the bread and wine lose their substance and natural essence, and that they are annihilated. They say they are changed into Christ’s body, and only the outward form remains.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶22, an error it rejects
NotesExplanation and background

Agreement and disagreement. Lutherans and Roman Catholics agree on the real presence. They differ on whether the bread and wine remain, whether the Mass is a sacrifice that earns forgiveness for others, and on giving only the bread.

Objection · Roman CatholicsThe Mass makes present Christ's one sacrifice and is offered for the sins of the living and the dead.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteChrist's sacrifice on the cross is the only sacrifice for sins. The Supper gives its benefits to those who receive it. It is not offered to God to apply that sacrifice to others' sins.

“This means that Christ is the only sacrifice applied on behalf of the sins of others. Therefore, in the New Testament, no sacrifice is left to be applied for the sins of others, except the one sacrifice of Christ upon the cross.”

Apology of the Augsburg Confession, Art. XXIV, ¶56
A false teaching the Confessions reject

“The papistic sacrifice of the Mass for the sins of the living and the dead.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶23, an error it rejects
NotesExplanation and background

Once for all. Hebrews 10:10-14.

Objection · Some ProtestantsOur faith is what makes Christ present.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteFaith receives Christ's gifts, but it doesn't create His presence. His word and power do.

A false teaching the Confessions reject

“Not the all-powerful words of Christ’s testament, but faith, produces and makes Christ’s body and blood present in the Holy Supper.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. VII, ¶35, an error it rejects

“For Christ does not say, “If you believe or are worthy, you receive My body and blood.” No, He says, “Take, eat and drink; this is My body and blood.””

Large Catechism, Sacrament of the Altar, ¶17
NotesExplanation and background

Why it matters. If faith created the presence, a struggling believer could never be sure what they received. Resting on Christ's word gives certainty.

Why some are saved and not others

Crux theologorum is Latin for “the theologians’ cross,” the hardest question in the doctrine of salvation: if God wants everyone to be saved, and salvation is entirely His work, why are some saved and not others? Calvinists answer by limiting God’s saving will to the elect. Arminians answer by tracing the difference to each person’s free response. Lutherans hold both things Scripture teaches and leave the rest to God. Taught this way, election is not a threat but a comfort: your salvation rests in God’s hands, not yours.

What the LCMS teaches

In the words of the Lutheran Confessions and the LCMS.

God wants everyone to be saved

In short summary, not a quoteGod's love is for the whole world. Christ died for all people, and God sincerely wants every person to come to faith and be saved.

“Scripture plainly reveals the truth that the love of God for the world of lost sinners is universal, that is, that it embraces all men without exception, that Christ has fully reconciled all men unto God, and that God earnestly desires to bring all men to faith, to preserve them therein, and thus to save them”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37

“just as the preaching of repentance is universal, so also the promise of the Gospel is universal, that is, it belongs to all people.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶28

Salvation is God's work alone

In short summary, not a quoteNo one contributes to becoming a Christian. Faith is entirely God's gift and work.

“faith in the Gospel, or conversion to God, is neither wholly nor in the least part the work of man, but the work of God's grace and almighty power alone”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶11

“The conversion of our corrupt will, which is nothing other than restoring it back to life from spiritual death, is only and solely God’s work”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. II, ¶87

Damnation is the person's own fault

In short summary, not a quoteWhen someone is lost, it is never because God didn't want them. It is because of their own sin and unbelief. Those who hear the Word and are lost have resisted it.

“The reason why not all who hear the Word believe, and some are therefore deeply condemned, is not because God had begrudged them their salvation. It is their own fault.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶78

“Many hearers of the Word indeed remain unconverted and are not saved, not because God does not earnestly desire their conversion and salvation, but solely because they stubbornly resist the gracious operation of the Holy Ghost”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶13

Election is to salvation only

In short summary, not a quoteGod chose His children for salvation from eternity. He did not choose anyone for damnation.

“Predestination, or God’s eternal election, covers only the godly, beloved children of God. It is a cause of their salvation, which He also provides.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. XI, ¶5

“But as earnestly as we maintain that there is an election of grace, or a predestination to salvation, so decidedly do we teach, on the other hand, that there is no election of wrath, or predestination to damnation.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37

Chosen by grace, not foreseen faith

In short summary, not a quoteGod didn't choose us because He saw something good in us, not even our future faith. Our faith is one of the gifts He chose to give.

“we reject as an anti-Scriptural error the doctrine that not alone the grace of God and the merit of Christ are the cause of the election of grace, but that God has, in addition, found or regarded something good in us which prompted or caused Him to elect us”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶36

“Nor does Holy Scripture know of an election "by foreseen faith," "in view of faith," as though the faith of the elect were to be placed before their election; but according to Scripture the faith which the elect have in time belongs to the spiritual blessings with which God has endowed them by His eternal election.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶36

God works through Word and Sacrament

In short summary, not a quoteGod calls and converts people through His Word and Baptism, and keeps them in faith through the Word and the Lord's Supper. He does not work apart from these means.

“we hold with Scripture that God offers and communicates to men the spiritual blessings purchased by Christ, namely, the forgiveness of sins and the treasures and gifts connected therewith, only through the external means of grace ordained by Him.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶21

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶76

Likely questions

  1. What is the "crux theologorum"?

    In short summary, not a quoteIt is the question of why some are saved and not others, when God wants all to be saved and salvation is entirely His work. Lutherans confess both truths and say Scripture doesn't answer the "why."

    “As to the question why not all men are converted and saved, seeing that God's grace is universal and all men are equally and utterly corrupt, we confess that we cannot answer it. From Scripture we know only this: A man owes his conversion and salvation, not to any lesser guilt or better conduct on his part, but solely to the grace of God. But any man's non-conversion is due to himself alone … it is the result of his obstinate resistance against the converting operation of the Holy Ghost”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶14

    “Calvinists solve this mystery, which God has not revealed in His Word, by denying the universality of grace; synergists, by denying that salvation is by grace alone. … every poor sinner stands in need of, and must cling to, both the unrestricted universal grace and the unrestricted "by grace alone," lest he despair and perish.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶16
    NotesExplanation and background

    The name. Crux theologorum means "the cross of theologians": the question every theology of salvation has to face. Lutheran teachers also call it cur alii prae aliis, "why some rather than others?"

    Two answers Lutherans reject. Calvinism says God never intended to save the rest, which denies that His grace is for all 1 Timothy 2:4. Arminianism traces the difference to each person's free response to enabling grace. Lutherans answer that this makes something in us the deciding factor, which conflicts with salvation by grace alone Ephesians 2:8-9.

    What Lutherans keep. Whoever is saved is saved by God's grace alone. Whoever is lost is lost by their own fault. Beyond that, Lutherans don't build a theory Deuteronomy 29:29.

  2. What if I'm afraid I'm not one of the elect?

    In short summary, not a quoteLook away from yourself and your feelings to Christ. His promise is for you, and He gave it to you personally in Baptism. The fear that you might not be chosen is a temptation, not a message from God.

    “Faith in this truth leaves no room for the fear that God might still harbor thoughts of wrath and damnation concerning us.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶40

    “It gives the most enduring consolation to all troubled, afflicted people, so that they know their salvation is not placed in their own hands.”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶90

    “If, by the grace of God, you believe in the Gospel of the forgiveness of your sins for Christ's sake, you are to be certain that you also belong to the number of God's elect”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶40
    NotesExplanation and background

    These thoughts come to believers. The Formula of Concord says Satan usually attacks godly hearts with these very thoughts (Solid Declaration XI ¶70). Worrying about whether you are chosen is not evidence that you aren't.

    Don't read the answer from your feelings. Believers sometimes can't feel God's presence at all. The Formula of Concord points to David, who said "I am cut off from your sight," and then, "But you heard the voice of my pleas for mercy" Psalm 31:22. "Whenever our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart" 1 John 3:19-20.

    Look where God points you. Christ says "whoever comes to me I will never cast out" John 6:37. "Who shall bring any charge against God's elect? … Who is to condemn? Christ Jesus is the one who died" Romans 8:31-34.

    Promises with your name on them. In Baptism God put His name on you personally. In the Lord's Supper Christ says "given for you." In Absolution you are forgiven by Christ's command. The Formula of Concord points troubled Christians to these: the Sacraments that seal the promise to each believer, private Absolution, and the grace promised in Baptism (Solid Declaration XI ¶37–38, ¶72; Epitome XI ¶13).

    Weak faith is still faith. "A bruised reed he will not break, and a faintly burning wick he will not quench" Isaiah 42:3. The Formula of Concord says that faith in the elect is often very weak, yet they still hunger and thirst for righteousness (Solid Declaration XI ¶30).

    You don't have to carry this alone. Helping troubled consciences is part of a pastor's work. LCMS congregations offer private confession and absolution, where you hear God's forgiveness spoken to you by name.

  3. Does God want everyone to be saved?

    In short summary, not a quoteYes. God earnestly wants every person to believe and be saved, and Christ died for the sins of the whole world.

    “Scripture plainly reveals the truth that the love of God for the world of lost sinners is universal, that is, that it embraces all men without exception, that Christ has fully reconciled all men unto God, and that God earnestly desires to bring all men to faith, to preserve them therein, and thus to save them”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37

    “This does not mean that God is unwilling to save everybody. … But their wickedness is responsible for this ‹that they perish›, not God or His election”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. XI, ¶12

    “just as the preaching of repentance is universal, so also the promise of the Gospel is universal, that is, it belongs to all people.”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶28
    NotesExplanation and background

    Scripture says so directly. God "desires all people to be saved" 1 Timothy 2:4. He is "not wishing that any should perish" 2 Peter 3:9. He has "no pleasure in the death of the wicked" Ezekiel 33:11.

    Christ died for all. He is "the propitiation … for the sins of the whole world" 1 John 2:2 and "gave himself as a ransom for all" 1 Timothy 2:3-6. Peter even speaks of false teachers "denying the Master who bought them" 2 Peter 2:1.

    Why it matters. If God's will to save were only for some, no one could be sure the Gospel is meant for them. Because it is for all, it is for you John 3:16.

  4. If salvation is all God's work, why isn't everyone saved?

    In short summary, not a quoteScripture gives two answers and no third. Those who are saved owe it entirely to God's grace. Those who are lost are lost because they resisted Him. Why one and not the other is not revealed.

    “As to the question why not all men are converted and saved, seeing that God's grace is universal and all men are equally and utterly corrupt, we confess that we cannot answer it. From Scripture we know only this: A man owes his conversion and salvation, not to any lesser guilt or better conduct on his part, but solely to the grace of God. But any man's non-conversion is due to himself alone … it is the result of his obstinate resistance against the converting operation of the Holy Ghost”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶14

    “The reason why not all who hear the Word believe, and some are therefore deeply condemned, is not because God had begrudged them their salvation. It is their own fault.”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶78
    NotesExplanation and background

    Both halves are in one passage. In Pisidian Antioch, the Jews who refused the Gospel "judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life," while among the Gentiles "as many as were appointed to eternal life believed" Acts 13:46-48. Unbelief is traced to the unbelievers, faith to God.

    Jesus' lament. "How often would I have gathered your children together … and you were not willing!" Matthew 23:37. Christ wanted to gather them, and they refused.

    Not a difference in people. The saved are not better or less resistant than the lost. All were "dead in the trespasses and sins" by nature Ephesians 2:1-5. That's why Lutherans won't say the saved "did their part."

  5. Do Lutherans believe in predestination?

    In short summary, not a quoteYes, in predestination to salvation. From eternity God chose His children in Christ, and that choice is a cause of their coming to faith and being kept in it.

    “Predestination, or God’s eternal election, covers only the godly, beloved children of God. It is a cause of their salvation, which He also provides.”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. XI, ¶5

    “all those who by the grace of God alone, for Christ's sake, through the means of grace, are brought to faith, are justified, sanctified, and preserved in faith here in time, that all these have already from eternity been endowed by God with faith, justification, sanctification, and preservation in faith, and this for the same reason, namely, by grace alone, for Christ's sake, and by way of the means of grace.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶35
    NotesExplanation and background

    It's in Scripture. God "chose us in him before the foundation of the world" Ephesians 1:3-6. "Those whom he predestined he also called" Romans 8:28-30.

    Election includes the means. God chose to save His elect through the Gospel and the Sacraments: "God chose you … to be saved, through sanctification by the Spirit and belief in the truth. To this he called you through our gospel" 2 Thessalonians 2:13-14.

    A comfort, not a puzzle. The Formula of Concord treats election as good news: our salvation rests in God's hands, not ours John 10:27-29, Romans 8:38-39.

  6. Does God predestine anyone to hell?

    In short summary, not a quoteNo. There is no election to damnation. God doesn't want anyone to be lost, and no one is lost because God planned it.

    “But as earnestly as we maintain that there is an election of grace, or a predestination to salvation, so decidedly do we teach, on the other hand, that there is no election of wrath, or predestination to damnation.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37

    “Eternal election is a cause why the elect are brought to faith in time, Acts 13:48; but election is not a cause why men remain unbelievers when they hear the Word of God.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37

    “Therefore, we reject the following errors: … God is unwilling that everyone should be saved.”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. XI, ¶16, ¶19
    NotesExplanation and background

    Where Lutherans and Calvinists part. Many Reformed confessions teach that God passed over the non-elect or ordained them to everlasting death. The Lutheran Confessions reject any teaching that God is unwilling to save some.

    Hardening is a judgment on resistance. The Formula of Concord says God determined to harden and condemn those who reject the Word and resist the Spirit (Solid Declaration XI ¶40, ¶83–85). It never says He decided that anyone should be lost apart from their own sin (see the objection about Pharaoh below).

    Vessels of wrath. Paul says God "endured with much patience vessels of wrath prepared for destruction," but He "prepared beforehand" only the vessels of mercy Romans 9:20-23.

  7. Doesn't God already know who will be saved?

    In short summary, not a quoteYes. God knows everything in advance. But knowing something will happen is different from causing it. God's foreknowledge doesn't cause anyone's unbelief.

    “This foreknowledge extends over the godly and the wicked alike. But it is not the cause of evil or of sin.”

    Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. XI, ¶4

    “Eternal election is a cause why the elect are brought to faith in time, Acts 13:48; but election is not a cause why men remain unbelievers when they hear the Word of God.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37
    NotesExplanation and background

    Two different things. The Formula of Concord distinguishes God's foreknowledge, which covers everything, good and evil, from His election, which covers only His children and actually causes their salvation.

    God knows the number. The Confessions grant that God knows exactly who will believe and who won't. He hasn't revealed it, and we aren't told to look for it Deuteronomy 29:29.

  8. Did God choose me because He knew I would believe?

    In short summary, not a quoteNo. God didn't choose anyone because He foresaw their faith or goodness. Faith is one of the gifts He chose to give them.

    “Nor does Holy Scripture know of an election "by foreseen faith," "in view of faith," as though the faith of the elect were to be placed before their election; but according to Scripture the faith which the elect have in time belongs to the spiritual blessings with which God has endowed them by His eternal election.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶36

    “we reject as an anti-Scriptural error the doctrine that not alone the grace of God and the merit of Christ are the cause of the election of grace, but that God has, in addition, found or regarded something good in us which prompted or caused Him to elect us”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶36
    NotesExplanation and background

    Faith comes from election. Acts says "as many as were appointed to eternal life believed" Acts 13:46-48, not "as many as believed were appointed."

    "Foreknew" has people as its object. "Those whom he foreknew" Romans 8:28-30: Paul speaks of God knowing people beforehand, not of foreseeing their faith.

    A history in the LCMS. In the late 1870s and 1880s C. F. W. Walther and the Missouri Synod rejected election "in view of faith" (intuitu fidei). Most 17th-century Lutheran theologians had used the phrase, meaning faith as the means of receiving Christ, but Missouri held that its opponents used it to make something in us the deciding factor in election. Ohio left the Synodical Conference in 1881. The Norwegian Synod withdrew in 1883 to settle the dispute within its own ranks, and its anti-Missouri minority left it in 1887. The Brief Statement (1932) restates the Missouri Synod's position.

  9. Do I decide to accept Christ?

    In short summary, not a quoteNo. By nature we are spiritually dead and can't choose God. The Holy Spirit creates faith through the Word. Believing is His gift.

    “faith in the Gospel, or conversion to God, is neither wholly nor in the least part the work of man, but the work of God's grace and almighty power alone”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶11

    “we reject every kind of synergism, that is, the doctrine that conversion is wrought not by the grace and power of God alone, but in part also by the co-operation of man himself”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶12

    “The conversion of our corrupt will, which is nothing other than restoring it back to life from spiritual death, is only and solely God’s work”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. II, ¶87
    NotesExplanation and background

    Dead people don't choose. We were "dead in the trespasses and sins" until God "made us alive together with Christ" Ephesians 2:1-5. The natural person "is not able to understand" the things of the Spirit 1 Corinthians 2:14.

    God gives the willing. "It is God who works in you, both to will and to work" Philippians 2:12-13. The Lord opened Lydia's heart to pay attention Acts 16:14. No one can say "Jesus is Lord" except by the Holy Spirit 1 Corinthians 12:3.

    What about "choosing"? After conversion a Christian's renewed will truly wants God's will and works with the Spirit. The Confessions deny that the unconverted will contributes anything to becoming a Christian. Even after conversion we cooperate only by the Spirit's power, not as an equal partner (Solid Declaration II ¶65–66).

    Synergism. From the Greek for "working together." It is the view that conversion is partly God's work and partly ours. The Formula of Concord (Article II) and the Brief Statement reject it.

  10. If God does it all, can anyone resist Him?

    In short summary, not a quoteYes. God's grace works through the Word, and people can and do resist it. God doesn't force anyone to believe.

    “God does not force a person to become godly. … Yet God the Lord draws the person whom He wants to convert [John 6:44].”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. II, ¶60

    “Many hearers of the Word indeed remain unconverted and are not saved, not because God does not earnestly desire their conversion and salvation, but solely because they stubbornly resist the gracious operation of the Holy Ghost”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶13
    NotesExplanation and background

    Scripture shows it. "You always resist the Holy Spirit" Acts 7:51. The Pharisees "rejected the purpose of God for themselves" Luke 7:30.

    Not a contradiction. A person can do nothing to bring about their conversion, but can still refuse it. By nature everyone resists. When someone stops resisting and believes, that too is God's work (Brief Statement ¶12). Those who are lost are the ones who kept resisting.

    Where Lutherans differ from Calvinists. Reformed theology teaches that the inward call to the elect can't finally be resisted. Lutherans teach that the same earnest grace comes to all who hear, and those who are lost resisted it.

  11. How can I know I am one of the elect?

    In short summary, not a quoteNot by searching God's hidden will or your own feelings, but by looking to Christ. If you believe the Gospel, you can be certain you are among God's elect.

    “Christians can and should be assured of their eternal election. … This assurance of one's personal election, however, springs only from faith in the Gospel”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶40

    “If, by the grace of God, you believe in the Gospel of the forgiveness of your sins for Christ's sake, you are to be certain that you also belong to the number of God's elect”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶40

    “Whoever would be saved should not trouble or torment himself with thoughts about God’s secret counsel, about whether he also is elected and ordained to eternal life.”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶70

    “Gaze upon the wounds of Christ and the blood shed for you; there predestination will shine forth.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶40, quoting Luther
    NotesExplanation and background

    Look at the promises. Christ says "whoever comes to me I will never cast out" John 6:37. "My sheep hear my voice … no one will snatch them out of my hand" John 10:27-29.

    Remember your Baptism. The Formula of Concord points troubled Christians to the grace God promised them in Baptism, and to the Sacraments, which seal the Gospel promise to each believer personally.

    Doubt is a temptation. The Formula of Concord calls fearful speculation about whether you are elected an attack of Satan, not a sign that you are lost.

  12. Can a true believer fall away?

    In short summary, not a quoteYes. Real believers can turn away and be lost. When that happens, it isn't because God stopped wanting to keep them, but because they turned from Him.

    “The reason is not that God was unwilling to grant grace for perseverance to those in whom He “began a good work,” for that is contrary to St. Paul (Philippians 1:6). The reason is that they willfully turn away again from the holy commandment, grieve and embitter the Holy Spirit, involve themselves again in the world’s filth, and redecorate their hearts as homes for the devil.”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶42
    NotesExplanation and background

    Jesus and Paul say so. Some "believe for a while, and in time of testing fall away" Luke 8:13. "Let anyone who thinks that he stands take heed lest he fall" 1 Corinthians 10:12. See also Hebrews 3:12-14.

    And yet the elect are kept. God's election "attains its end with all whom it embraces" (Brief Statement ¶38). This is a comfort for believers, not a reason to be careless Philippians 1:6.

    Different from "once saved, always saved." Many who hold that view point back to a moment of decision. Lutherans point to Christ's promise, received in faith today.

  13. If God has already chosen, why preach, evangelize, or go to church?

    In short summary, not a quoteBecause the Word and Sacraments are how God calls and keeps His elect. Election doesn't replace the means of grace; it works through them.

    “Furthermore, the declaration in John 6:44 is right and true, “No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him.” However, the Father will not do this without means, but has ordained His Word and Sacraments for this purpose as ordinary means and instruments.”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶76

    “all those who by the grace of God alone, for Christ's sake, through the means of grace, are brought to faith, are justified, sanctified, and preserved in faith here in time, that all these have already from eternity been endowed by God with faith, justification, sanctification, and preservation in faith, and this for the same reason, namely, by grace alone, for Christ's sake, and by way of the means of grace.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶35
    NotesExplanation and background

    How people come to faith. "How are they to believe in him of whom they have never heard?" … "faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ" Romans 10:13-17.

    Election includes the calling. "Those whom he predestined he also called" Romans 8:28-30. God's choice and His call through the Gospel go together 2 Thessalonians 2:13-14.

    No one should wait for a private sign. The Formula of Concord warns against despising the preached Word while waiting for God to draw a person some other way.

  14. Why do some people hear the Gospel and others never do?

    In short summary, not a quoteScripture tells us only part. God owes no one His Word, and where He removes it, that is a deserved judgment. Beyond that, His judgments are beyond us. What He has told us is to take the Gospel to all nations.

    “God does not owe us the Word, the Spirit, or grace.”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶60

    “we enter the domain of the unsearchable judgments of God and ways past finding out, which are not revealed to us in His Word, but which we shall know in eternal life”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶15
    NotesExplanation and background

    Paul stops here too. "Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments" Romans 11:33-36.

    What we do know. God wants all people saved 1 Timothy 2:4, and faith comes through hearing the Word Romans 10:13-17. That's why the Church sends missionaries.

    God's grace shown to us. The Formula of Concord says that seeing others without the Word should teach us to praise God's undeserved grace toward us, not to judge Him.

  15. Why don't Lutherans try to resolve the tension?

    In short summary, not a quoteBecause Scripture doesn't. Every attempt to explain it gives up one of the two truths. Lutherans would rather confess both and leave the mystery to God.

    “Blind reason indeed declares these two truths to be contradictory; but we impose silence on our reason. The seeming disharmony will disappear in the light of heaven, 1 Cor. 13:12.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶38

    “We cannot harmonize God’s revelation with our reason, which we have not been commanded to do anyway.”

    Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶53

    “Calvinists solve this mystery, which God has not revealed in His Word, by denying the universality of grace; synergists, by denying that salvation is by grace alone. … every poor sinner stands in need of, and must cling to, both the unrestricted universal grace and the unrestricted "by grace alone," lest he despair and perish.”

    LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶16
    NotesExplanation and background

    What each solution costs. If you explain it by God choosing only some, you lose "God wants all saved." If you explain it by our choice, you lose "by grace alone." A troubled sinner needs both.

    Not irrational, just limited. Lutherans don't claim the two truths contradict each other. They say we can't see how they fit, and won't until we see face to face 1 Corinthians 13:12.

    God's ways are higher. Isaiah 55:8-9, Deuteronomy 29:29.

Common objections

Objections come from both directions. Each is stated the way its holders would put it, followed by the Lutheran answer.

Objection · Reformed Christians (Calvinists)God chose some for salvation and passed over the rest, leaving them to damnation.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteLutherans agree that God chose His elect by grace alone. They reject that He chose or passed over anyone for damnation, because Scripture says He wants all to be saved.

“But as earnestly as we maintain that there is an election of grace, or a predestination to salvation, so decidedly do we teach, on the other hand, that there is no election of wrath, or predestination to damnation.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37

“Therefore, we reject the following errors: … God is unwilling that everyone should be saved.”

Formula of Concord, Epitome, Art. XI, ¶16, ¶19

“Calvinists solve this mystery, which God has not revealed in His Word, by denying the universality of grace; synergists, by denying that salvation is by grace alone. … every poor sinner stands in need of, and must cling to, both the unrestricted universal grace and the unrestricted "by grace alone," lest he despair and perish.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶16
NotesExplanation and background

The Reformed view. The Westminster Confession says that by God's decree "some men and angels are predestinated unto everlasting life; and others foreordained to everlasting death." It adds that God passed by the rest and ordained them to wrath "for their sin."

The Lutheran answer. The logic seems to require it: if God chose some, He must have rejected the others. But Scripture says God wants all to be saved 1 Timothy 2:4, Ezekiel 33:11. Lutherans let Scripture limit the logic, not the other way around.

Shared ground. Both agree that salvation is God's work from start to finish and that no one is chosen for their own merit Ephesians 1:3-6.

Objection · Five-point Calvinists (limited atonement)Christ died only for the elect.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteChrist died for all people. His death paid for the sins of the whole world, including those who reject Him.

“Scripture plainly reveals the truth that the love of God for the world of lost sinners is universal, that is, that it embraces all men without exception, that Christ has fully reconciled all men unto God, and that God earnestly desires to bring all men to faith, to preserve them therein, and thus to save them”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37

“just as the preaching of repentance is universal, so also the promise of the Gospel is universal, that is, it belongs to all people.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶28
NotesExplanation and background

Scripture's "all." Christ "gave himself as a ransom for all" 1 Timothy 2:3-6. He is the propitiation "for the sins of the whole world" 1 John 2:2. False teachers deny "the Master who bought them" 2 Peter 2:1.

The common reply. Calvinists often read "all" as "all kinds of people." But in 1 Timothy 2 Paul has just urged prayers "for all people," kings included, and the same "all" runs through the passage.

Why it matters. If Christ didn't die for everyone, a preacher can't tell any particular person "Christ died for you." Lutherans can say it to anyone.

Objection · Reformed Christians (Calvinists)God's grace is irresistible. Everyone He truly calls will come.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteGod's grace is powerful enough to convert anyone, but He works through the Word and doesn't force people. Scripture shows people resisting the Holy Spirit.

“God does not force a person to become godly. … Yet God the Lord draws the person whom He wants to convert [John 6:44].”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. II, ¶60

“Many hearers of the Word indeed remain unconverted and are not saved, not because God does not earnestly desire their conversion and salvation, but solely because they stubbornly resist the gracious operation of the Holy Ghost”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶13
NotesExplanation and background

Resistance in Scripture. "You always resist the Holy Spirit" Acts 7:51. "How often would I have gathered your children … and you were not willing!" Matthew 23:37.

One call, not two. Reformed theology distinguishes an outward call to all from an effectual inward call to the elect, while saying the outward call is sincere. Lutherans answer that the Formula of Concord rejects the idea that God calls all outwardly but in His heart means it for only a few (Solid Declaration XI ¶34–35). The Brief Statement adds that the grace offered to those who are lost is "altogether earnest and fully efficacious for conversion" (¶38).

The same grace. Lutherans say the grace offered to those who are lost is as earnest and powerful as the grace that converts the saved. The difference lies in their resistance, not in God's offer.

Objection · Arminians, Methodists, many evangelicals, and some Lutherans historicallyGod elected those He foresaw would believe.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteFaith is not the reason for election but one of its fruits. God chose us by grace alone, and the faith we have is part of what He chose to give.

“Nor does Holy Scripture know of an election "by foreseen faith," "in view of faith," as though the faith of the elect were to be placed before their election; but according to Scripture the faith which the elect have in time belongs to the spiritual blessings with which God has endowed them by His eternal election.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶36

“we reject as an anti-Scriptural error the doctrine that not alone the grace of God and the merit of Christ are the cause of the election of grace, but that God has, in addition, found or regarded something good in us which prompted or caused Him to elect us”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶36
NotesExplanation and background

Romans 8:29. "Those whom he foreknew" has persons as its object: Paul speaks of God knowing people beforehand, not of foreseeing their decision Romans 8:28-30. Acts 13:48 has faith following God's appointing, not the reverse Acts 13:46-48.

What it would cost. If election rested on foreseen faith, the final difference between saved and lost would be something in us. That brings back the boasting Paul excludes Ephesians 2:8-9.

Shared ground. Lutherans agree with Arminians that God wants all people saved and that grace can be resisted. They disagree that anything in us is the cause of our election.

Objection · Arminians, Wesleyans, and many evangelicalsGod gives everyone the ability to choose; whether we are saved depends on our free decision.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteWe have no power to choose God. Conversion is entirely the Holy Spirit's work through the Word, though people can reject it.

“we reject every kind of synergism, that is, the doctrine that conversion is wrought not by the grace and power of God alone, but in part also by the co-operation of man himself”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶12

“The conversion of our corrupt will, which is nothing other than restoring it back to life from spiritual death, is only and solely God’s work”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. II, ¶87
NotesExplanation and background

Prevenient grace. Wesleyan Arminians teach that God's grace restores to everyone the ability to accept or reject Christ. Lutherans agree grace goes first, but say it doesn't stop at enabling us. It creates faith itself Philippians 2:12-13.

Dead in sin. Scripture calls us dead before conversion Ephesians 2:1-5, Colossians 2:13, and the natural mind hostile to God Romans 8:7.

Comfort. If salvation hung on the quality of my decision, I could never be sure I'd decided well enough. Lutherans point to what God has done, not what I chose.

Objection · Many evangelicals"Behold, I stand at the door and knock" shows we have to open the door.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteChrist does knock through His Word, and His knocking is a real invitation. But the power to open comes from the same Spirit who calls.

“Furthermore, the declaration in John 6:44 is right and true, “No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him.” However, the Father will not do this without means, but has ordained His Word and Sacraments for this purpose as ordinary means and instruments.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶76

“faith in the Gospel, or conversion to God, is neither wholly nor in the least part the work of man, but the work of God's grace and almighty power alone”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶11
NotesExplanation and background

The context. Revelation 3:20 is addressed to the church in Laodicea, a church that had grown lukewarm. Jesus calls them to repent and promises fellowship. It isn't a description of how unbelievers first come to faith.

Invitations are real. Scripture is full of genuine invitations Matthew 11:28, and Lutherans preach them. The ability to respond is God's gift Philippians 2:12-13, 1 Corinthians 12:3.

"Choose this day." Joshua's call Joshua 24:15 was spoken to Israel, a people God had already redeemed. God's commands show what we should do, not what we can do without Him Ezekiel 18:31, Ezekiel 36:26.

Objection · Christian universalistsIf God wants everyone saved and nothing can stop Him, everyone will be saved.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteGod's will to save all is real, but people can resist it. Scripture says plainly that some will be lost.

“Many hearers of the Word indeed remain unconverted and are not saved, not because God does not earnestly desire their conversion and salvation, but solely because they stubbornly resist the gracious operation of the Holy Ghost”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶13

“while the universal will of grace is frustrated in the case of most men, Matt. 22:14; Luke 7:30, the election of grace attains its end with all whom it embraces, Rom. 8:28-30.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶38
NotesExplanation and background

Some are lost. "these will go away into eternal punishment" Matthew 25:46. "Whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life" John 3:36, Mark 16:16.

A will that can be refused. God's saving will works through the Word, and Jesus says some "were not willing" Matthew 23:37.

Shared ground. Lutherans agree with universalists that God loves every person and that Christ died for all. They disagree that everyone will therefore be saved.

Objection · Many Baptists and Reformed Christians (eternal security)Once you are truly saved, you can never be lost.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteReal believers can fall away. God never stops wanting to keep them, but people can turn their backs on Him.

“The reason is not that God was unwilling to grant grace for perseverance to those in whom He “began a good work,” for that is contrary to St. Paul (Philippians 1:6). The reason is that they willfully turn away again from the holy commandment, grieve and embitter the Holy Spirit, involve themselves again in the world’s filth, and redecorate their hearts as homes for the devil.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶42
NotesExplanation and background

Scripture's warnings. Some "believe for a while, and in time of testing fall away" Luke 8:13. "Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away" Hebrews 3:12-14. See also 1 Corinthians 10:12 and Galatians 5:2-4.

The Baptist and Reformed view. Many Baptists, and Reformed Christians under the name "perseverance of the saints," teach that anyone who truly believes will persevere, and that someone who falls away was never truly saved. Lutherans take the warnings as describing a real danger: a true believer can fall away and be lost.

Real assurance. Lutherans have strong assurance. It rests on Christ's promises in Word and Sacrament, received in faith John 10:27-29, rather than on looking back to a moment of decision.

Objection · Many Reformed ChristiansGod hardened Pharaoh's heart, so God decides who is hardened.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteGod did harden Pharaoh's heart, but as a judgment after Pharaoh had repeatedly hardened himself against God's warnings. It doesn't show that God wanted him lost.

“God did harden Pharaoh’s heart. In other words, Pharaoh always sinned again and again and became more hardened the more he was warned.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶85
NotesExplanation and background

Read the order. Exodus repeatedly says Pharaoh hardened his own heart Exodus 8:15. The narrative first says the Lord hardened it after the sixth plague (Exodus 9:12), though God foretold it at the start Exodus 4:21.

Paul's point. The Formula of Concord reads Romans 9:17-18 as showing God's justice toward those who despise His Word, and says Paul doesn't teach that God had ordained Pharaoh to damnation.

Objection · Many Reformed ChristiansRomans 9 teaches that God makes some people vessels of wrath.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quotePaul says God prepared the vessels of mercy for glory. He says God patiently endured the vessels of wrath, not that He made them for destruction.

“Here, then, the apostle clearly says that God endured with much long-suffering the vessels of wrath. But He does not say that He made them vessels of wrath.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶80

“Eternal election is a cause why the elect are brought to faith in time, Acts 13:48; but election is not a cause why men remain unbelievers when they hear the Word of God.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶37
NotesExplanation and background

The wording differs. Paul says God "prepared beforehand" the vessels of mercy, but only that the vessels of wrath were "prepared for destruction," without saying who prepared them Romans 9:20-23.

Jacob and Esau. "Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated" Romans 9:11-13 quotes Malachi, written centuries later about the nations descended from them. Paul uses it to show election is "not because of works but because of him who calls."

The potter. "Who are you, O man, to answer back to God?" Romans 9:20-23 silences the complaint that God is unfair to find fault when no one can resist His will (Romans 9:19). Lutherans read it as a limit on our questions, not as an explanation of who is lost.

Objection · Reformed Christians (Calvinists)"No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him" means only the chosen are drawn.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteNo one can come to Christ unless the Father draws them. The Father draws through His Word, and Jesus says He will draw all people.

“Furthermore, the declaration in John 6:44 is right and true, “No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him.” However, the Father will not do this without means, but has ordained His Word and Sacraments for this purpose as ordinary means and instruments.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶76
NotesExplanation and background

The same verb. The word for "draws" in John 6:44 is used again in John 12:32: "I … will draw all people to myself."

Lutherans agree with the first half. No one comes on their own power John 6:44. But the drawing happens through the Word, which is preached to all.

"Whoever comes." In the same chapter Jesus says "whoever comes to me I will never cast out" John 6:37.

Objection · Calvinists, Arminians, and some Lutheran criticsHolding both truths is a contradiction. You have to pick one.

What the sources say

In short summary, not a quoteLutherans don't claim to see how the two truths fit. They confess both because Scripture teaches both, and leave the rest to God.

“Blind reason indeed declares these two truths to be contradictory; but we impose silence on our reason. The seeming disharmony will disappear in the light of heaven, 1 Cor. 13:12.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶38

“We cannot harmonize God’s revelation with our reason, which we have not been commanded to do anyway.”

Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration, Art. XI, ¶53

“Calvinists solve this mystery, which God has not revealed in His Word, by denying the universality of grace; synergists, by denying that salvation is by grace alone. … every poor sinner stands in need of, and must cling to, both the unrestricted universal grace and the unrestricted "by grace alone," lest he despair and perish.”

LCMS, Brief Statement of the Doctrinal Position of the Missouri Synod (1932), ¶16
NotesExplanation and background

Seeming, not real. The Brief Statement calls it a "seeming disharmony." Lutherans don't say God contradicts Himself, only that He hasn't shown us how it fits 1 Corinthians 13:12.

Charges from both sides. Calvinists sometimes call the Lutheran view inconsistent. In the election controversy, other Lutherans accused Missouri of "Crypto-Calvinism." The Brief Statement answers that refusing to go beyond Scripture is not masked Calvinism (¶15), and it rejects synergism just as firmly (¶12, ¶16).

Paul's own response. Romans 11:33-36.

Where Lutherans, Calvinists, and Arminians agree

A summary, not an LCMS source. Lutherans share important convictions with both sides of this debate.

  • Every person is a sinner who needs a Savior Romans 3:23-24.
  • Salvation is by grace through faith in Christ Ephesians 2:8-9.
  • God knows all things before they happen.
  • Scripture teaches election, and Christians should take comfort in it, though each tradition defines it differently Ephesians 1:3-6.
  • The Gospel is to be preached to all people Matthew 28:19-20.
  • With Calvinists, Lutherans confess that salvation is God's work alone and that no one is chosen for anything in themselves.
  • With Arminians, Lutherans confess that God sincerely wants everyone to be saved, that Christ died for all, and that grace can be resisted.
