Infant Baptism Verses

Infant Baptism: The Verses

The Bible passages behind infant baptism, explained in the words of The Lutheran Church—Missouri Synod (LCMS).

How to use this page

This page does not try to settle the question. It shows how Lutherans read these passages, and it says where the LCMS documents give no answer.

Each card has a Bible passage, then the LCMS’s own words, quoted exactly, with the source under each quote. Have a question or an objection? Find it in the list below and tap it. The objections were worded for this page, as fairly as it could be done. Five keep wording printed by the LCMS and are labeled.

Three kinds of lines are not quotations. Note explains a word or links two quotes. Added fact gives a fact from outside the LCMS documents, such as a Greek word, a date, or what other churches hold. Not addressed marks a point on which these documents are silent.

Most quotes come from a 2018 report on Baptism by the LCMS’s theology commission, the CTCR, and from the LCMS’s published answers to readers’ questions, its FAQ (see Sources). The LCMS’s standard is Scripture. The Lutheran Confessions are quoted only to show what Lutherans confess. These documents hold that it is a mistake to withhold Baptism from infants, and the Confessions, and in places the FAQ, say so in sharp words. This page gives their reasons and leaves the sharp words out.

Jump to an objection or question (23)

1. God’s command and promise

  • ‘Baptizing them’ means baptizing people who are already disciples.
  • ‘Repent and be baptized.’ ‘Whoever believes and is baptized.’ Faith comes first.

2. Our need

  • Babies are not yet accountable for sin.
  • Each person has to choose Christ for himself or herself.

3. What Baptism gives

  • We are saved by faith, not by works. Baptism follows salvation.
  • Baptism is an outward sign of an inward change.
  • The thief on the cross was never baptized.
  • Cornelius received the Spirit before he was baptized.
  • 1 Peter 3:21 calls Baptism ‘an appeal to God.’ A baby cannot appeal.

4. Infants and faith

  • Jesus blessed these children. He did not baptize them.
  • Babies cannot believe. They do not know or understand anything yet.
  • Faith comes by hearing. A baby cannot understand the gospel.
  • How can you know a baby has faith?

5. Circumcision and households

  • Circumcision was for a physical household. The new covenant is for believers.
  • No verse shows a baby being baptized. In Acts, people believe first.

6. One Baptism

  • I came to faith later. Shouldn’t I be baptized now?
  • Many baptized as babies never show faith.
  • Does the LCMS accept a Baptism done in another church?

7. More questions and answers

  • Jesus was baptized as an adult.
  • Infant baptism came in after the apostles.
  • Doesn’t ‘baptize’ mean immerse? Jesus was immersed.
  • When does a child baptized as a baby profess his or her own faith?
  • What if the baby of Christian parents dies before it is baptized?
Words used on this page

LCMS: The Lutheran Church—Missouri Synod. “The Synod” means the same. The CTCR is its Commission on Theology and Church Relations.

Sacrament: a sacred act that Christ commanded, in which God’s promise is joined to something physical, such as water.

Means of grace: the ways God gives forgiveness and faith: His Word, Baptism, and the Lord’s Supper.

Original sin: the sinful condition every person is born with since Adam’s fall.

The old Adam, the old man: that sinful nature.

Regeneration: new birth; being born again.

Valid: real. In the Lutheran view a valid Baptism is a true Baptism and is not repeated.

Mode: the way the water is applied: immersing, pouring, or sprinkling.

Sponsors: godparents.

Confirmation: a public rite in which a person who was baptized as a baby, after being taught, confesses the faith for himself or herself.

Fides directa, fides reflexa: Latin terms in the 2018 report for two aspects of faith: trust in God, and knowledge of His work with agreement to His words.

The Lutheran Confessions: the Lutheran statements of faith from the 1500s, such as the Small and Large Catechisms, the Augsburg Confession, the Smalcald Articles, and the Formula of Concord. The LCMS holds that they explain Scripture correctly.

LC IV, KW: short forms used in the 2018 report. LC IV is the Large Catechism’s part on Baptism. KW is a modern English edition of the Lutheran Confessions.

LSB: in the quotations, Lutheran Service Book, the LCMS hymnal. In the translation menu at the top of the page, the Legacy Standard Bible.

Cf.: compare.

What the LCMS teaches

“Lutherans believe that the Bible teaches that a person is saved by God’s grace alone through faith in Jesus Christ alone. The Bible tells us that such ‘faith comes by hearing’ (Rom. 10:17). Jesus Himself commands Baptism and tells us that Baptism is water used together with the Word of God (Matt. 28:19-20). Because of this, we believe that Baptism is one of the miraculous means of grace (another is God’s Word as it is written or spoken), through which God creates and/or strengthens the gift of faith in a person’s heart”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism and its purpose”

“Baptism is a Sacrament, a physical means established by Christ to give salvation. In Baptism, the Word of God joined with the water drowns the old Adam, washing the Christian clean and naming him or her as a forgiven child of God. This is not by any action or merit on the part of the Christian, which is why Baptism is conferred on babies, children and adults alike in the Lutheran church.”

lcms.org, “What is Baptism, and what does it do?”

Note “Means of grace” are the ways God gives forgiveness and faith. “The old Adam” is the sinful nature every person is born with. “Conferred” means given.

“Lutherans do not believe that only those baptized as infants receive faith. Faith can also be created in a person’s heart by the power of the Holy Spirit working through God’s (written or spoken) Word. Baptism should then soon follow conversion (cf. Acts 8:37) for the purpose of confirming and strengthening faith in accordance with God’s command and promise. Depending on the situation, therefore, Lutherans baptize people of all ages from infancy to adulthood.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism and its purpose”

Note “Cf.” means compare. So the FAQ says that adults who come to faith are baptized too. The difference with churches that baptize only believers is over infants and young children, and over what Baptism is.

Added fact Acts 8:37 is in the KJV and NKJV. Most newer Bibles bracket it or move it to a footnote.

How the LCMS makes its case

Note The 2018 report says the debate turns on what Baptism is.

“When considering infant Baptism, the key questions are: What is Baptism, according to Scripture? What does Baptism actually do?”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “What are the biblical and theological reasons for baptizing infants?”

“The central question of the entire debate can be expressed in a straightforward manner. Is Baptism primarily God’s promise to us or is Baptism primarily our promise to God? If Baptism is God’s promise to adopt us and to be our heavenly Father, there is no reason that God cannot make a promise to an infant as well as to an adult. It is analogous to parents who adopt an infant child. They promise to care for the child apart from that child’s ability to assent.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “What are the biblical and theological reasons for baptizing infants?”

Note That is how the report puts the question, and the LCMS holds the first view. “Assent” means agreement.

Added fact Churches that baptize only believers usually describe their own view differently: Baptism is an act of obedience by a person who has already come to faith.

Note The FAQ gives the LCMS’s reasons under three headings. Parts 1 to 3 below follow them.

  • “Lutherans baptize infants because of what the Bible teaches regarding:”
  • “God’s command to baptize”
  • “Our need for Baptism”
  • “God’s promises and power”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why baptize infants?”

Note About the baptisms recorded in the New Testament, the FAQ says:

“Whole households, everyone in the family, were baptized in the beginning of New Testament times, which in all probability included infants (Acts 16:15 and 33).”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Infant Baptism history”

Note None of the documents quoted on this page points to a verse that names an infant being baptized. The FAQ and the report rest their case on the command to baptize, on our need, on what Baptism is and gives, on whom the promise includes, and on the baptisms of whole households. The Large Catechism also argues from the Christians God has made of people who were baptized as infants.

1. God’s command and promise

“Make disciples of all nations, baptizing them” Matthew 28:19–20

19Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, 20teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.”ESV

See it in context

What the LCMS says

“Jesus Himself has commanded: ‘Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them … [and] teaching them’ (Matt. 28:19–20) — an all-encompassing mandate which excludes no age group.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “What are the biblical and theological reasons for baptizing infants?”

“There is not a single passage in Scripture which instructs us not to baptize for reasons of age … On the contrary, the divine commands to baptize in Scripture are all universal in nature.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why baptize infants?”

“The word ‘nations’ in Matt. 28:19 refers to outsiders, indeed to all people — all those who live outside of God’s gracious reign and who are ruled and controlled by destructive desires and forces.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 1, “Baptism: God’s Invitation into the Kingdom of God”

Note A “mandate” is a command.

Objection“Jesus said to make disciples and baptize them. ‘Them’ means disciples: people who have already heard and believed. A baby is not yet a disciple.”
The LCMS answers

“our Lord combines Baptism and teaching in Matthew 28 as He explains how we are to make disciples. We should never separate what He joins together.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “When can or should adult converts be baptized?”

“The church has always sought to hold Christ’s command to baptize and teach together. But it has done so in various ways and, at times, with different sequences. As noted earlier, in the early church’s missionary setting, most Baptisms were adult Baptisms. In this situation, teaching often came first and was then followed by Baptism. … As the Baptism of infants within Christian families became more common (and eventually the norm), the ordering shifted so that Baptism came first and instruction followed.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 4, “How Is Teaching Carried Out in Connection with Baptism?”

“Since those baptized are also to be instructed in the Christian faith, (Matt. 28:20), the church baptizes infants only where there is the assurance that parents or spiritual guardians will nurture the faith of the one baptized through continued teaching of God’s Word.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why baptize infants?”

Note So the report reads “make disciples” as something done by baptizing and teaching together, in either order. It agrees that with adult converts teaching usually comes first.

Not addressed These documents take the nations to be the ones baptized (see the FAQ quote on the next card). They do not discuss the other reading, the Greek, or John 4:1, where Jesus is said to be “making and baptizing” disciples.

“The promise is for you and for your children” Acts 2:38–39

38And Peter said to them, “Repent and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. 39For the promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off, everyone whom the Lord our God calls to himself.”ESV

See it in context

What the LCMS says

“Infants are included in ‘all nations’ who are to be baptized (Matt. 28:19). Certainly they were included in Peter’s Pentecost exhortation in Acts 2:38, 39”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Infant Baptism history”

“This is why Christian parents bring their babies for Baptism as soon as possible: because of the clear promises of God even for children (Acts 2:39), who certainly also need a Savior from sin and death (Rom. 6:23).”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “What if the baby of Christian parents dies before it is baptized?”

“Of the baptism of children we hold that children ought to be baptized. For they belong to the promised redemption made through Christ, and the Church should administer it [Baptism and the announcement of that promise] to them.”

What Lutherans confess: Smalcald Articles, Part III, Article V

Note An “exhortation” is an urging. Pentecost is the day Peter preached this sermon. How far “your children” reaches is one of the questions listed at the end of this page.

Objection“Peter said ‘Repent and be baptized,’ and Jesus said ‘Whoever believes and is baptized will be saved’ (Mark 16:16). Repentance and faith come first, and a baby can do neither.”
The LCMS answers

Note The FAQ answers a reader who raised this objection: “The Bible says that repentance is a pre-requisite for faith. I repented at 5, so it can be early, but not in someone’s arms.” It first cites Matthew 28:19 and Acts 2:38–39, then says:

“Baptism through the Word creates the faith necessary to receive salvation for infants.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Infant Baptism history”

“Faith may precede Baptism (as with adult converts); faith may be created in and through Baptism; faith may be sustained through the promise after Baptism. But it is the promise of God that is central in each case.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 4, “What Is the Purpose of God’s Promises to Us in Baptism?”

“He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved. That is, faith alone makes the person worthy to receive profitably the saving, divine water.”

What Lutherans confess: Large Catechism, Baptism, paragraph 33

Note So these documents agree that faith is needed, and that an adult convert believes first and is then baptized. Their claim is that God gives an infant faith through Baptism (Part 4).

Not addressed These documents do not discuss repentance by infants as a separate question.

2. Our need

“In sin did my mother conceive me” Psalm 51:5

5Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me.ESV

See it in context

What the LCMS says

“According to the Bible, all people–including infants–are sinful and fall short of the glory of God (Rom. 3:23). King David confesses, ‘I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me’ (Ps. 51:5). Like adults, infants die–sure proof that they too are under the curse of sin and death.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why baptize infants?”

Note This teaching is called original sin. Whether David speaks here for every infant is one of the questions listed at the end of this page.

Objection“A baby may be born with a sinful nature, but God does not hold little children accountable until they know right from wrong.”
The LCMS answers

“In a word, Jesus died and rose in order to be their gracious Lord as well. By Baptism, they are taken out of their previous life as aliens and outsiders and brought under Jesus’ protection and blessing. Even an infant stands in great need of such rescue: ‘The Word of God also teaches that we are all conceived and born sinful and are under the power of the devil until Christ claims us as His own. We would be lost forever unless delivered from sin, death, and everlasting condemnation’ (Rite of Holy Baptism, LSB, p. 268; see Ps. 51:5; Eph. 2:1–3; Acts 26:18; Col. 1:13).”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “What are the biblical and theological reasons for baptizing infants?”, with words from the LCMS rite of Holy Baptism

Note “Their” means infants: the report is answering why they are baptized. LSB here is Lutheran Service Book, the LCMS hymnal.

Not addressed These documents do not discuss an age of accountability by that name, or the passages often cited for it (Deuteronomy 1:39; 2 Samuel 12:23). A note in the report lists, among teachings the Lutheran Confessions reject, that unbaptized children are innocent in God’s sight and are saved without Baptism (see the last question on this page).

Note For the baby of Christian parents who dies before Baptism, these documents hold out hope. They do not say the same of every child. See “What if the baby of Christian parents dies before it is baptized?”

“Born of water and the Spirit” John 3:5–6

5Jesus answered, “Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God. 6That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit.ESV

See it in context

What the LCMS says

“Jesus’ dramatic encounter with Nicodemus (John 3) is one of the most well-known New Testament accounts of how God’s promise in Baptism makes us members of God’s kingdom, akin to a ‘birth certificate’ that attests to our citizenship within God’s kingdom. In the course of their conversation, Jesus declares: ‘Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God’ (John 3:5). Noteworthy here is the connection of water and the Spirit with the imagery of birth.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 2, “New Birth and Life”

Note The FAQ lists John 3:5–7 under the heading “Our need for Baptism” (the second of its three reasons). Whether “water” here means Baptism is one of the questions listed at the end of this page.

Objection“A person has to choose Christ for himself or herself. A baby cannot choose, and parents cannot choose for a child.”
The LCMS answers

“With regard to how He creates faith, we can say this much: God does not force someone to believe. We can also say this: God does not leave it to our decision (as our will is bound by sin).”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “When and how does Baptism create faith?”

“When a child is too young to answer for himself or herself, the sponsors speak on the child’s behalf and as representatives of Christ’s Body, the Church, renouncing Satan and expressing the Church’s faith in the Triune God. As the sponsors answer the baptismal questions of renunciation and confession on behalf of the child, this expresses verbally the faith into which the child is to be raised and mentored.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 4, “What Other Elements Might Be Present in the Service?”, on sponsors

Note “Our will is bound by sin” means that a sinner cannot choose God by his or her own power. The report says this of adults as well as infants. “Sponsors” are godparents. The adoption comparison near the top of this page also bears on the last part of the objection.

Note Not every church that baptizes only believers would word the objection this way. For the objection that faith comes by hearing, see “Faith comes by hearing. A baby cannot understand the gospel.”

3. What Baptism gives

Note The FAQ lists the Bible passages on what Baptism gives and sums them up in one sentence. “These passages” are the ones named in the sentence itself, with Mark 16:16. Tap a reference to read it.

“these passages describe Baptism as a gracious and powerful work of God through which He miraculously (though through very ‘ordinary’ means) washes away our sins by applying to us the benefits of Christ’s death and resurrection (Acts 2:38-39; Acts 22:16), gives us a new birth in which we ‘cooperate’ just as little as we did in our first birth (John 3:5-7), clothes us in Christ’s righteousness (Gal. 3:26-27), gives us the Holy Spirit (Titus 3:5-6), saves us (1 Peter 3:21), buries us and raises us up with Christ as new creatures (Rom. 6:4; Col. 2:11-12), makes us holy in God’s sight (Eph. 5: 25-26) and incorporates us into the body of Christ (1 Cor. 12:13). All of this, according to the Bible, happens in Baptism, and all of it is God’s doing, not ours.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why baptize infants?”

“The washing of regeneration” Titus 3:4–7

4But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, 5he saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit, 6whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus Christ our Savior, 7so that being justified by his grace we might become heirs according to the hope of eternal life.ESV

See it in context

What the LCMS says

“Jesus was not alone in using the language of new birth or rebirth to describe the new beginning initiated by Baptism.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 2, “New Birth and Life”

“Martin Luther speaks of this regeneration as a ‘washing of the new birth in the Holy Spirit.’ This new birth makes new creatures, members of a new creation.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 2, “New Birth and Life”

“For without the word of God the water is simple water and no baptism. But with the word of God it is a baptism, that is, a gracious water of life and a washing of regeneration in the Holy Ghost, as St. Paul says, Titus, chapter three”

What Lutherans confess: Small Catechism, Baptism, “How can water do such great things?”

Note The first quote introduces the report’s quotation of these verses. “Regeneration” means new birth. Whether “washing” here means water Baptism is one of the questions listed at the end of this page.

Objection“We are saved by grace through faith, not by works (Ephesians 2:8-9). Baptism is an act of obedience that follows salvation. It does not bring it about.”
The LCMS answers

“This loving God did not institute Baptism as something one has to do to be saved (a work of the Law); rather, He gave Baptism as a means of salvation (a gift of the Gospel).”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “What if the baby of Christian parents dies before it is baptized?”

“In Baptism, God gives us His Word, a Word that bestows the benefits of Christ’s death and resurrection. ‘God has given us His word on it!’ But the promises of God’s Word seek faith and are only embraced by faith. This is what we mean when we say sola fide.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum, “What is the relationship between Baptism and faith?”

“Yes, our works, indeed, avail nothing for salvation; Baptism, however, is not our work, but God’s … God’s works, however, are saving and necessary for salvation, and do not exclude, but demand, faith; for without faith they could not be apprehended.”

What Lutherans confess: Large Catechism, Baptism, paragraph 35

Note Sola fide is Latin for “by faith alone.” “Apprehended” means taken hold of. So these documents agree that we are saved by grace through faith and not by works. Their claim is that Baptism is God’s work and gift, which faith receives.

“Buried… with him by baptism into death” Romans 6:3–4

3Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? 4We were buried therefore with him by baptism into death, in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life.ESV

See it in context

What the LCMS says

“In Romans 6, Paul provides us with the most comprehensive treatment of how Baptism interweaves our story with Christ’s”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 3, “Baptism Promises Freedom from Death and the Devil”

“Paul’s primary emphasis here is that Baptism unites us with Christ’s resurrection. For if Christ had not been raised, there would be no reason to talk about the significance of His death — much less the importance of Baptism.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 3, “Baptism Promises Freedom from Death and the Devil”

“In Romans 6, the Holy Spirit tells us in the Word that in Baptism we have been united with Jesus’ death and resurrection–regenerated, dying to sin and rising to new life. That happens to infants when baptized (Gal. 3:27).”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Infant Baptism history”

Objection“Baptism is a picture of what has already happened to a believer. It is an outward sign of an inward change and a public profession of faith.”
The LCMS answers

“None of the passages listed above, nor any passage in Scripture, describes Baptism as ‘our work’ or as ‘our public confession of faith.’”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why baptize infants?”

“When the apostle Paul recounted his pre-conversion life in which he persecuted and killed followers of Jesus, he recalled how Ananias told him: ‘And now why do you wait? Rise and be baptized and wash away your sins, calling on his name’ (Acts 22:16).”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 3, “Baptism Promises the Forgiveness of Sins”

“the Sacraments were ordained, not only to be marks of profession among men, but rather to be signs and testimonies of the will of God toward us, instituted to awaken and confirm faith in those who use them.”

What Lutherans confess: Augsburg Confession, Article XIII

Note “The passages listed above” are those in the FAQ’s summary at the top of this part. For the phrase in 1 Peter 3:21 that some translations give as “pledge,” see the last objection on the next card. The Augsburg Confession’s words are “not only”: it grants that the Sacraments are also public marks of a Christian’s profession. Its claim is that they are first God’s act toward us.

Note The 2018 report warns against turning attention from what God does in Baptism to what people do. That can happen, it says, “if we see Baptism as little more than the outward response to an inward reality” (Addendum).

“Baptism… now saves you” 1 Peter 3:20–21

20because they formerly did not obey, when God's patience waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared, in which a few, that is, eight persons, were brought safely through water. 21Baptism, which corresponds to this, now saves you, not as a removal of dirt from the body but as an appeal to God for a good conscience, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ,ESV

See it in context

What the LCMS says

Note The FAQ’s summary at the top of this part cites this verse for the words “saves us.” The 2018 report comments on Peter’s comparison with the flood. “These two dimensions” are God’s sweeping away of evil and His making all things new.

“These two dimensions of the same event are expressed by Peter with reference to the flood and Noah’s ark as an analogy for Baptism, noting God’s use of the power of water both to destroy and to save.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 3, “Baptism Promises Safe Passage through the Final Judgment”

“It works forgiveness of sins, delivers from death and the devil, and gives eternal salvation to all who believe this, as the words and promises of God declare.”

What Lutherans confess: Small Catechism, Baptism, “What does Baptism give or profit?”

Objection“If Baptism saves, what about the thief on the cross? He was never baptized.”
The LCMS answers

“The LCMS does not believe that Baptism is ABSOLUTELY necessary for salvation. All true believers in the Old Testament era were saved without baptism. Mark 16:16 implies that it is not the absence of Baptism that condemns a person but the absence of faith, and there are clearly other ways of coming to faith by the power of the Holy Spirit (reading or hearing the Word of God). Still, Baptism dare not be despised or willfully neglected, since it is explicitly commanded by God and has His precious promises attached to it.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism and its purpose”

“we teach that it is not the lack of Baptism that necessarily condemns, but it is the despising of this precious gift that endangers faith, for God Himself has instituted it and attached His promises to it.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism saves?”

Note God has tied us to Baptism, but He has not tied Himself. In words the FAQ quotes from LCMS theologian Otto Sohn: “God is not Himself bound by the means to the use of which He has bound us.” The Augsburg Confession calls Baptism “necessary to salvation” (Article IX); the FAQ’s word “absolutely” marks the limit.

Not addressed None of these documents mentions the thief on the cross (Luke 23:43). Their general answer applies to him.

Objection“Cornelius and his household received the Holy Spirit before they were baptized (Acts 10:44-48). People are saved first and baptized afterward.”
The LCMS answers

Note The FAQ answers a reader who made this point from Acts 10:44–48. Its reply is general:

“while Baptism is God’s gracious means of conveying to human beings His saving grace revealed to us in Jesus Christ our Savior, it is not the only means.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism saves?”

“It is no less a miracle of God’s grace at work that an adult should believe by hearing the words of the Gospel, than that an infant should receive through Baptism the Spirit who creates the very faith by which one receives incorporation into Christ”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism saves?”

“Baptism confirms the grace of God upon adults who have already come to faith, and strengthens them in their faith, even as the Lord’s Supper does.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism saves?”

Note So the FAQ agrees that an adult can come to faith before Baptism. It says Baptism then confirms and strengthens that faith.

Not addressed The FAQ’s reply does not discuss Acts 10 itself, and none of these documents discusses the order of events there.

Objection“Peter calls Baptism ‘an appeal to God for a good conscience.’ That is something a believer does. A baby cannot make an appeal.”
Related LCMS passages

Not addressed None of these documents explains the phrase “an appeal to God for a good conscience,” so there is no direct answer in them to quote. The two passages below are related, but they were not written about this verse.

“God’s forgiveness not only has the power to re-establish our relationship with Him, it also has the power to give us a clear conscience again. It enables us to feel clean again — on the basis of His sure and certain Word and promise — so that we need not feel shame before God and seek to hide from Him as Adam and Eve did (Gen. 3:8–10). This gift of forgiveness in Baptism is highlighted by the vivid image of washing.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 3, “Baptism Promises the Forgiveness of Sins”

“It is not the water indeed that does them, but the word of God which is in and with the water, and faith, which trusts such word of God in the water.”

Question, in the LCMS’s own wording“Are infants or people who have mental or emotional limitations capable of believing the promises of Baptism?”
The LCMS answers

“Yes. When raising such a question, however, we must be careful not to shift our focus away from what God does in Baptism to the question of whether we (as adults or children) are intellectually, volitionally or emotionally capable of believing.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum

“Having said that, it may be helpful to distinguish between two aspects of faith, namely, faith as fides directa and faith as fides reflexa. The former refers to our orientation toward God that is characterized by trust and confidence. The latter refers to our knowledge of God’s work and agreement with His words and promises.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum

“To maintain that infants are given faith in Baptism does not imply that they intellectually grasp all that this entails (knowledge) or volitionally acknowledge it as true (assent). “As Luther notes, ‘We bring the child with the intent and hope that it may believe, and we pray God to grant it faith. For this reason, we presume that the Spirit creates faith in infants because God is able to do what He promises (Romans 4). In a similar vein, Paul declares, ‘For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ’ (Gal. 3:27). It is hard to imagine how one could be clothed with Christ without faith.”

Note The report’s word is “presume.” “Volitionally” means by an act of the will. “Assent” means agreement.

6. One Baptism

“One Lord, one faith, one baptism” Ephesians 4:4–6

4There is one body and one Spirit—just as you were called to the one hope that belongs to your call— 5one Lord, one faith, one baptism, 6one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in all.ESV

See it in context

What the LCMS says

“The Scriptures teach, of course, that there is only one Baptism (Eph. 4:5). There is no indication that God has limited this blessed means of grace to individuals on the basis of age or levels of maturity. Baptism is God’s act, a divine testimony to what ‘grace alone’ really means, whereby He imparts the blessings of forgiveness, life, and salvation to individuals, children and adults alike.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism saves?”

“In Baptism, we are united with Christ and thereby also united with every other baptized Christian. We share the same promises and the same hope as part of the same Body of Christ. Herein lies our indissoluble unity as Christians.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 2, “Baptism Makes Us Members of a New Community”

“‘One Baptism’ highlights the dramatic once-and-for-all nature of Baptism that transitions us to a totally new and wondrous life and future.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 3, “Baptism Promises the Forgiveness of Sins”, on the Nicene Creed’s words “one Baptism for the remission of sins”

Note The Nicene Creed is a statement of faith from the 300s that most Christian churches share. “Remission” means forgiveness.

Objection“I was baptized as a baby, but I did not believe until years later. Shouldn’t I be baptized now that I believe?”
The LCMS answers

“even though Christ’s promises in Baptism call for and engender faith, faith does not make this particular washing with water a Baptism. Only God’s name and promise do that.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 4, “What Is the Purpose of God’s Promises to Us in Baptism?”

“even though infants did not believe, which, however, is not the case, yet their baptism as now shown would be valid, and no one should rebaptize them”

What Lutherans confess: Large Catechism, Baptism, paragraph 55

“if you did not believe then believe now and say thus: The baptism indeed was right, but I, alas! did not receive it aright. For I myself also, and all who are baptized, must speak thus before God: I come hither in my faith and in that of others, yet I cannot rest in this, that I believe, and that many people pray for me; but in this I rest, that it is Thy Word and command.”

What Lutherans confess: Large Catechism, Baptism, paragraph 56

“These statements simply follow Paul’s argument about Abraham and his faith in the promise. … ‘No unbelief made him waver concerning the promise of God, but he grew strong in his faith as he gave glory to God, fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised’ (Rom. 4:20–21).”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 4, “What Is the Purpose of God’s Promises to Us in Baptism?”

Note “Engender” means produce. “Aright” means rightly. “These statements” are the words of Luther in the quote before it, from “I come hither” on, which the report gives in a modern translation.

Note So in the Lutheran view a person who comes to faith later is receiving what his or her Baptism already promised, and should not be baptized again. The claim is about God’s promise in the first Baptism. It says nothing about the sincerity of a person who was later baptized again.

Objection“Many people who were baptized as babies never show any faith. If Baptism gave them new birth, where did it go?”
The LCMS answers

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Baptism and its purpose”

Question, in the LCMS’s own wording“Do I have to be re-baptized to join the LCMS?”
The LCMS answers

“The LCMS recognizes and accepts the validity of baptisms properly administered (i.e., using water in any quantity and/or mode, together with the Trinitarian invocation instituted by Christ, Matt. 28:19) in all Christian churches. Assuming, therefore, that you have already received a proper Christian Baptism, there would be no need for you or your wife to be re-baptized, although completion of some form of ‘instruction classes’ or ‘membership classes’ is normally required of non-Lutherans who wish to become communicant members of LCMS congregations.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Do I have to be re-baptized to join the LCMS?”

“Only the Word of God and the ‘element’ (water), according to divine institution, makes a Baptism valid.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why do Lutherans use the ‘sprinkle’ method for Baptism?”

Objection“Infant baptism came in after the apostles. There is no clear record of it before about AD 200, and the first writer to mention it argued against it.”
The LCMS answers

“the Christian church has baptized infants from the earliest days of its history.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 4, “Who Is to Be Baptized?”

Early Christian writers

Added fact The New Testament does not name an infant being baptized (see Part 5). The writers below lived after the apostles. They are evidence of what early Christians did, not proof from Scripture. Of the three quotes, only Origen’s comes from an LCMS document. The dates and comments in these lines are the page’s own, and they claim less than the FAQ does.

“And so, according to the circumstances and disposition, and even age, of each individual, the delay of baptism is preferable; principally, however, in the case of little children. … The Lord does indeed say, Forbid them not to come unto me. Let them come, then, while they are growing up; let them come while they are learning, while they are learning whither to come; let them become Christians when they have become able to know Christ.”

Tertullian, On Baptism, chapter 18, about AD 200. Not an LCMS document

Added fact Tertullian wrote in North Africa. He is the first writer who plainly mentions the baptism of little children, and he advised putting it off. So the practice was known by about AD 200, and it had a critic.

“Origen wrote in his Commentary on Romans 5:9: ‘For this also it was that the Church had from the Apostles a tradition to give baptism even to infants.’”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Infant Baptism history”, historical excerpts from Dr. Dennis Kastens

“But in respect of the case of the infants, which you say ought not to be baptized within the second or third day after their birth, and that the law of ancient circumcision should be regarded, so that you think that one who is just born should not be baptized and sanctified within the eighth day, we all thought very differently in our council.”

Cyprian of Carthage, Epistle 58 (64 in modern editions), about AD 253. Not an LCMS document

Added fact Origen wrote in the 240s. This commentary survives in a Latin translation made about AD 405. Cyprian wrote for a council of sixty-six bishops in North Africa. The question put to them was not whether to baptize infants but whether to wait until the eighth day.

Added fact The FAQ also says that Polycarp “was baptized as an infant” (early writers call him a disciple of the apostle John). At his death, about AD 155, he said, “Eighty and six years have I served Him” (The Martyrdom of Polycarp, chapter 9). The FAQ’s statement is an inference from that number. No record of his Baptism exists.

Added fact Those who baptize only believers point to other early evidence. The Didache (about AD 100) and Justin Martyr (about AD 155) describe the Baptism only of people who have been taught and believe. Several church leaders of the 300s who were raised by Christian parents, such as Gregory of Nazianzus and Basil the Great, were baptized as adults. The FAQ cites the Didache and Justin for other points. These documents do not discuss this evidence.

Question, as printed in the LCMS FAQ“The people of the Bible, including Jesus, were baptized using the immersion method. Why doesn’t our church follow the way Jesus was baptized by John?”
The LCMS answers

“On the basis of the evidence provided in the New Testament, it is not possible to prove that the term ‘baptize’ always refers to immersion, nor that the Baptisms mentioned were all done by immersion”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why do Lutherans use the ‘sprinkle’ method for Baptism?”

“For example, the practice of immersing a person beneath the water vividly captures the symbolism of being buried (beneath the water) with Christ and rising up with Christ (Rom. 6:1–4). Thus Luther himself notes that Baptism is the act or ceremony that ‘consists of being dipped into the water, which covers us completely, and being drawn out again’ (LC IV 65). Some maintain that Baptisms during Jesus’ day took place by immersing a person completely beneath the water. Others argue that such was probably not the case since the waters in the river Jordan were often too low to allow for immersion. Both positions are arguing from silence. The Bible simply does not say, which is good and sufficient reason for the church not to insist on a particular mode of Baptism.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Part 4, “How Is the Water to Be Applied in Baptism?”, quoting Luther’s Large Catechism in part

“Lutherans have therefore held that the manner of Baptism (that is, immersion, pouring, sprinkling, etc.) does not determine whether a Baptism is valid, any more than the manner of distributing the Lord’s Supper (common cup, individual glasses) affects the validity of this Sacrament.”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “Why do Lutherans use the ‘sprinkle’ method for Baptism?”

Note The FAQ goes further than the first quote: “In fact, taken as a whole, the evidence suggests otherwise.” “For example” follows the report’s remark that reasons have been given for applying the water in particular ways. So these documents accept immersion as a true Baptism. What they do not accept is that immersion is required.

Not addressed These documents do not discuss the Greek dictionaries, which give “dip” and “immerse” among the word’s meanings, or what the passages usually cited for immersion show (Mark 1:10; John 3:23; Acts 8:38–39). The 2018 report takes the word to mean washing with water in any of these ways, and the FAQ points to Mark 7:4, where the same Greek word is used for washing.

Question, in the LCMS’s own wording“What if the baby of Christian parents dies before it is baptized?”
The LCMS answers

“When this happens, Christian parents have wonderful reasons to hope for the salvation of their child. They know who their God is: the One who sent His own Son to die on the cross to redeem and save little children, no less than others (2 Cor. 5:19). The all-loving God loves their little one!”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum

“Yet the absence of Baptism, in itself, does not cause someone to be lost. We know that God has formed this child in the womb and truly desires it to be saved.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum

“When a child dies before Baptism, the Lutheran pastor will earnestly seek to bring to grieving parents comfort and care from the Lord. He will always be ready to assure Christian parents that they may have hope that their child has been in the loving care of their gracious God, the Father of our Savior, Jesus Christ.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), Addendum

“This answer applies to parents who earnestly desired to baptize their children and did not despise the saving promises of Baptism. It should therefore not be misunderstood as a denial of either the efficacy or general necessity of infant Baptism or of the concerns raised in the Formula of Concord, Solid Declaration Article XII, paragraphs 11–13.”

LCMS CTCR, An Inexpressible Treasure (2018), note 25

“‘There is some basis for the hope that God has a method, not revealed to us, by which He works faith in the children of Christians dying without Baptism (Mark 10:13-16). For children of unbelievers we do not venture to hold out such hope. We are here entering the field of the unsearchable judgments of God’ (Romans 11:33).”

LCMS, Frequently Asked Questions: Doctrine, “What about infants who die before being baptized?”, quoting Otto Sohn, who quotes Francis Pieper

Note “Efficacy” is Baptism’s power to do what God promises. The Formula of Concord is one of the Lutheran Confessions. The report’s note goes on to say that the paragraphs named reject three teachings: that unbaptized children are innocent in God’s sight and are saved without Baptism; that children should not be baptized until they can confess the faith themselves; and that the children of Christians are God’s children without and before Baptism. Francis Pieper was an LCMS president and teacher.

Not addressed The report limits its answer to parents who wanted Baptism for their child. It does not say what comfort it would offer Christian parents who, out of conviction, do not baptize infants.

Not answered in these documents

None of the documents quoted on this page takes up these objections:

  • “Born of water” in John 3:5 and “the washing of regeneration” in Titus 3:5 do not mean water Baptism. The documents read both as Baptism and do not discuss other readings.
  • “Your children” in Acts 2:39 means descendants, and the promise is only for those “whom the Lord our God calls to himself.”
  • The “little ones who believe” in Matthew 18:6 are older children or humble disciples, not babies. “To such” in Luke 18:16 means people who are like children (verse 17).
  • In the household passages the households hear, believe, or serve (Acts 10:44–48; Acts 16:32–34; Acts 18:8; 1 Corinthians 16:15).
  • Psalm 22:9 and Psalm 51:5 are David’s poetry about himself, and the unborn John (Luke 1:41–44) was a special case. They are not statements about every infant.
  • The new covenant is made with people who know the Lord (Jeremiah 31:31–34), so its sign belongs to believers.
  • A person who is truly born again cannot fall away (John 10:28), so Baptism cannot give new birth to people who later show no faith.

They are fair questions, and worth talking through together.
